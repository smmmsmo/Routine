# Sugar, Tea, Coffee & Drinks — Practical Nutrition Guide

**Purpose:** Improve body composition, cardiovascular health, sleep and workout recovery without unnecessarily banning tea or coffee.  
**Context:** You already avoid Mojo, Coke and similar soft drinks — keep that habit. This guide focuses on sugar hidden in everyday drinks and snacks.

## 1. How Much Sugar per Day?

| Reference | Limit | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| **Practical daily goal** | **Around 0–25 g free sugar** | A useful, stricter target for your goals; not a medical requirement to eat exactly 25 g |
| **WHO** | **Less than 10% of daily calories**; ideally **5% or less** | At **2,000 kcal/day**, roughly **less than 50 g**, ideally around **25 g or less** of *free sugars* |
| **AHA added sugar limit** | Men: **36 g/day**; women: **25 g/day** | Covers *added sugar*, a narrower category than WHO free sugar |

**1 level teaspoon table sugar ≈ 4 g sugar ≈ 16 kcal.**  
Example: **2 cups of tea × 2 teaspoons added sugar each = 16 g sugar** before counting sweets, biscuits or any other sweet drinks.

**Important:** The WHO 50 g illustration assumes a 2,000-calorie diet; your actual energy requirement may differ. Less free sugar is generally better than regularly approaching the upper limit. You do not need to consume sugar just to meet a target.

### What counts as sugar?

| Item | Counts toward free/added sugar limit? | Explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Sugar added to tea/coffee | **Yes** | White/brown sugar are still sugar |
| Honey, gur/jaggery, syrups | **Yes** | Natural origin does not remove free-sugar concerns |
| Sweetened condensed milk | **Yes** | Added sugar; check the label |
| Packaged juice, even 100% fruit juice | **Yes — free sugars** | Whole fruit is a better everyday choice |
| Whole banana, apple, guava or orange | **No** (whole-fruit intrinsic sugar) | Comes with fibre and other nutrients |
| Plain milk, unsweetened yogurt | **No** for natural lactose | But flavoured/sweetened versions may contain added sugar |
| Plain rice, roti, oats | **Not free sugar** | Still contain carbohydrates and calories, so portions matter |

## 2. Which Drinks Should You Choose?

| Drink | Recommendation | Practical preparation |
|---|---|---|
| **Plain water** | **Best everyday option** | Drink regularly; add water around exercise |
| **Black tea** | **Fine** | Prefer 0 sugar; reduce from 1 tsp to ½ tsp then 0 if desired |
| **Milk tea (দুধ চা)** | **Fine in moderation** | Use normal milk or unsweetened milk powder; little/no added sugar |
| **Black coffee** | **Fine** | No sugar required |
| **Milk coffee / latte** | **Fine** | Plain milk + coffee, avoid sweet syrups |
| **Lemon water** | **Fine** | Water + lemon, without sugar or syrup |
| **Plain lassi / unsweetened yogurt drink** | **Fine** | Yogurt + water + spices, no added sugar |
| **Packaged fruit juice / sweetened smoothies** | **Limit** | Prefer whole fruit and plain water |
| **3-in-1 coffee sachets, bottled sweet coffee, bubble tea** | **Usually limit** | Often substantial added sugar; inspect label |
| **Energy drinks** | **Generally avoid** | Can add a lot of caffeine and/or sugar; not necessary for a 45-min workout |
| **Soda / Coke / Mojo** | **Continue avoiding** | You're already doing well without these |

### You do NOT need to quit tea or coffee

For most healthy adults, the **FDA cites up to 400 mg caffeine/day** as an amount not generally linked to harmful effects, but **it is a limit reference, not a goal**. Caffeine content varies widely by cup size and brewing.

**Practical suggestion:** One or two moderate cups of tea/coffee daily if they suit your sleep and stomach. Prefer them earlier in the day; avoid caffeine in the **6–8 hours before bedtime** if it disrupts sleep. Some people are sensitive even to smaller doses. If you have palpitations, significant anxiety, reflux or trouble sleeping, reduce or skip it.

You do not need caffeinated pre-workout drinks or energy drinks for your evening exercise.

## 3. Your Tea & Coffee Rules

1. **Milk tea:** Tea + milk/unsweetened milk powder; ideally **0–½ tsp sugar** per cup. If used to sweeter tea, reduce gradually.
2. **Black tea:** Tea + water; lemon/ginger optional. Avoid using honey/gur as an imagined “sugar-free” alternative.
3. **Coffee:** Coffee + water or plain milk; avoid sweetened condensed milk, syrup and sugary 3-in-1 mixes most days.
4. **Homemade cold coffee:** Coffee + plain cold milk + ice; avoid ice cream, flavoured syrups and large amounts of sugar.
5. **With snacks:** Pair drinks with fruit, unsalted nuts, egg or plain yogurt instead of automatically adding biscuits/cakes every time.

## 4. What to Drink Around Night Exercise?

| When | What |
|---|---|
| Before | Normal water, based on thirst and conditions |
| During | Water if thirsty; no sports drink needed for a typical 45-minute session |
| After | Water; eat a normal balanced meal |
| Close to bedtime | Water or a caffeine-free drink if wanted |

There is **no universal exact litre target** that works for everyone; heat, sweat, body size and meals change fluid needs. Avoid forcing excessive water. Dark urine, thirst and heavy sweating are cues to pay attention to hydration.

## 5. Read Labels Without Getting Tricked

- Check **serving size** first: the bottle may contain more than one serving.
- Check **“Added Sugars”** when available; **“Total Sugars”** includes natural milk/fruit sugars too.
- Common sweetener names: sugar, syrup, glucose, fructose, sucrose, dextrose, honey, jaggery, fruit juice concentrate.
- **“No added sugar” ≠ no sugar**; fruit juice may still contain free sugars.
- **“Brown sugar,” “organic sugar,” “honey” and “gur” are not free-sugar loopholes.**
- More coffee does not compensate for lost sleep.

## 6. Easy Everyday Example

| Item | Added/free sugar |
|---|---:|
| Morning milk tea, no sugar | ~0 g added |
| Afternoon tea, 1 level tsp sugar | ~4 g |
| Plain coffee in the day | ~0 g added |
| 1 small sweet snack (variable) | Read portion/label |
| **Total from tea and coffee above** | **~4 g** |

This leaves room for an occasional dessert without turning each drink into a sugar source. Whole fruit and plain milk **do not** need to be counted as added sugar.

**Bottom line:** Keep tea and coffee if you enjoy them, favour **0–25 g/day free sugar** as a practical goal, keep soda out as you already do, and protect sleep.

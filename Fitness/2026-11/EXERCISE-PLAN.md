# November exercise — six days, Friday recovery

## 1. Structure and effort

Train **Saturday through Thursday**, with a short morning session and a main afternoon session. This combines three non-consecutive strength days with three walking days. The cardio, strength, hip movement and breathing together support physical stamina and general sexual fitness; a separate hard sexual-health workout every day is not required.

Choose a repeatable afternoon slot, for example 5 PM. Allow a large meal to settle before training. Use a non-slip floor, a wall and, if necessary, a stable counter or chair secured against a wall. No gym equipment or improvised hanging/table rows are needed.

**Moderate walking:** breathing is quicker, but you can still talk in sentences; singing would be difficult. Use the talk test rather than chasing a heart-rate number. **Strength:** end each set while you could still perform about two more controlled repetitions. Breathe out during the effort; avoid straining and breath-holding.

## 2. M — the short morning routine, about 10 minutes

| Order | Movement | Time / amount |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Easy march in place, relaxed arm movement | 1 minute |
| 2 | Brisk march in place or brisk walk outside | 5 minutes at moderate effort |
| 3 | Gentle standing hip-flexor stretch, then calf/ankle movement | 30 seconds each hip, then 1 minute ankle movement |
| 4 | Comfortable belly/rib breathing | 2 minutes; inhale gently, let the abdomen/pelvic floor soften, exhale without bearing down |

During marching, keep steps low and quiet. Faster arm movement can raise effort without jumping. If five brisk minutes is too much initially, alternate one minute brisk/one minute easy; record only the genuinely moderate minutes.

Morning work should leave you refreshed. Hard squats, push-ups and long planks belong on strength days, allowing those muscles to recover between sessions.

## 3. Afternoon strength sessions — usually 30–40 minutes

Start with **3 minutes of easy marching, arm circles and comfortable practice squats**. Complete the listed sets, then walk briskly for **10 minutes**, and finish with **2 minutes easy walking/breathing**. Rest **45–75 seconds between sets**, longer if needed for breathing to settle. The session may take up to 45 minutes if you need longer rests; do not rush to hit a timer.

### Technique and easier options

| Movement | How to do it | Easier option |
|---|---|---|
| Sit-to-stand / squat | Feet comfortable width, sit hips back, knees follow toes, stand through the whole foot. Use a stable chair against a wall. | Higher seat or a shallower squat, with light hand support |
| Wall/counter push-up | Hands around chest height, body straight, bend elbows under control and push away; shoulders stay comfortable. | Use the wall and stand closer |
| Glute bridge | Lie with knees bent, feet planted. Gently lift hips by squeezing the buttocks; stop before the lower back arches. | Smaller lift |
| Prone W | Lie face down, forehead supported on a folded towel, elbows bent into a W. Gently lift hands/elbows a little, draw shoulder blades together, pause 1–2 seconds. Keep chest low. | Standing shoulder-blade squeeze |
| Bird dog | On hands/knees, reach opposite arm and leg without twisting or sagging. Small, controlled reach. | Move only an arm or slide one leg back |
| Reverse lunge | Lightly hold a secure support, step one foot back, bend only as far as comfortable, return through the front foot. | Sit-to-stand or supported split-stance shallow knee bend |
| Self-resisted row | Reach left arm forward, hold its wrist with the right hand. Pull left elbow back towards the ribs against gentle right-hand resistance; slowly return. Repeat other arm. No jerking. | Very light resistance; substitute standing shoulder-blade squeezes if uncomfortable |
| Dead bug | Lie on back, knees bent above hips, arms up. Keep ribs comfortable and lower back steady while lowering one heel to tap the floor. Alternate. | Start with both feet on the floor and slide one heel away |
| Calf raise | Lightly hold a wall, lift heels, pause, lower slowly. | Both hands supported, smaller range |
| Hip hinge | Soft knees, move hips back with a long neutral back, then stand by bringing hips forward. | Stand facing away from a nearby wall and gently reach hips back to touch it |
| Side plank, knees | Elbow beneath shoulder, knees bent, gently lift hips with trunk aligned. | Shorter hold or a standing side plank with forearm against a wall |

The row and W raise provide a starting back stimulus, but equipment-free pulling is limited. Judge progress by controlled effort, not a promise that these replace heavy rows indefinitely.

## 4. Afternoon walking and monthly progression

Every walking session includes **5 minutes easy at the start + the brisk block below + 5 minutes easy at the end**. Walk on a comfortable level route. If outside walking is impractical, use indoor walking/marching with the same talk-test intensity.

| Dates | Brisk block on Sun/Tue/Thu | Whole walking session | Strength progression | Approx. moderate aerobic minutes in a full 7-day block* |
|---|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 1–7 Nov | 25 min | 35 min | Start at the first-week amounts; use one set where needed | 135 |
| 8–14 Nov | 30 min | 40 min | Two work sets if recovery is good | 150 |
| 15–21 Nov | 30 min | 40 min | Add 1–2 reps within the listed ranges | 150 |
| 22–28 Nov | 35 min if comfortable, otherwise stay at 30 | 40–45 min | Keep two sets; make only one comfortable progression | 150–165 |
| 29–30 Nov | Sunday 30–35 min brisk; Monday normal Strength B | As above | Maintain, then review | Partial week |

*Includes 5 moderate morning minutes × 6 days, 10 brisk minutes after strength × 3 days, and the three main brisk blocks. Warm-ups, easy cool-downs and breathing are not counted. If a block is easy rather than moderate, log actual moderate minutes. Strength minutes are tracked separately.*

If 35 minutes total is initially too long, start with your comfortable 30 minutes and build gradually. Repeat a week's level whenever needed; the target is not an obligation to push through pain or exhaustion.

Once all sets reach the top of a range with good control for two sessions, change **one** thing: slightly lower the push-up support, slow a squat's lowering to 3 seconds, or add 5 seconds to the side plank, up to 25 seconds. Keep repetitions controlled and avoid adding every progression at once. No maximal tests or all-out sprints this month.

## 5. Sexual fitness — what to do on all six active days

The daily foundations are the **morning brisk movement and relaxed breathing**, plus that day's afternoon workout. Walking builds aerobic capacity; strength develops the hips, legs and trunk used during sustained activity. General activity and healthy weight management can support erectile health. The hips/core exercises are general conditioning, not proven cures for sexual symptoms.

### Optional pelvic-floor practice — about 1 extra minute

This is **optional**. NIDDK advises checking suitability with a health professional first. Until then, use breathing/relaxation alone; being symptom-free does not establish that your pelvic floor needs strengthening.

If suitable and you can identify and release the muscles comfortably:

1. Sit or lie down, breathe normally, and gently lift as though preventing gas. Keep the abdomen, thighs and buttocks relaxed; do not bear down.
2. Do **5 gentle repetitions: 3 seconds contraction, at least 6 seconds fully relaxed**, once on Saturday–Thursday. Keep this dose throughout November; Friday is off. It is introductory practice, not a validated sexual-performance prescription.
3. Do not practise by repeatedly interrupting urination or clench throughout other exercise.

Stop for pelvic pain/pressure, painful ejaculation or difficulty starting urination; seek clinical advice. Persistent sexual symptoms need assessment rather than more repetitions.

## 6. Recovery and practical rules

Aim for consistent sleep, generally 7–9 hours. During long study/computer sessions, stand and move for a couple of minutes about every hour. These easy breaks help reduce sitting; they are extra to the planned brisk activity.

Some mild muscle soreness can follow new exercise. If soreness changes your movement, reduce sets or use easy walking that day. Sharp pain, joint pain or radiating back pain means stop the provoking exercise. Stop exercise and seek urgent help for chest pain, fainting or unusual severe breathlessness.

If you miss a session, resume the next scheduled session. Do not combine two hard strength workouts or train through Friday to repay a missed day. Record actual work honestly. Use [TRACKER.md](TRACKER.md) to decide the next step.

# November food plan — one person, ৳6,000

## 1. Starting intake and measurements

Start with the portions below for 14 days. They usually provide about **2,100–2,300 kcal and 90–120 g protein/day**; some fish/chicken days or richer recipes can be higher. This is a budget-conscious starting plan, not a measured energy requirement or an athletic high-protein prescription. Use the trend rules in [TRACKER.md](TRACKER.md) to adjust it.

**All rice, atta, dal and soy amounts are DRY, before cooking. Vegetables and fruit in meals are weighed after removing inedible parts, before cooking. Fish/chicken serving weights are cooked edible flesh, excluding bones and gravy.** The shopping table instead uses purchase weight.

If there is no kitchen scale, ask the grocer to weigh ingredients and divide them into equal daily packets. Calibrate your own cup/bowl once using a known amount. A cup's volume does not equal the same weight for rice, dal and flour.

## 2. Every day's food allocation

| Food | Daily total | How to use it |
|---|---:|---|
| Ordinary rice | **180 g dry** | Cook for the day; divide equally between lunch and dinner |
| Whole-wheat atta | **80 g dry** | Two rotis using 40 g flour each; no oil/ghee in dough or brushed on top |
| Eggs | **2 whole eggs** | Breakfast; boiled, poached or cooked using some of the daily oil allowance |
| Masoor/mung dal | **80 g dry** | 40 g dry equivalent at lunch + 40 g at dinner; water does not replace lentils |
| Plain dry soy chunks | **40 g dry** | Cook thoroughly following packet instructions; normally at dinner |
| Plain cow's milk | **250 ml** | Drink it or divide between tea/coffee; count all milk together |
| Unsalted peanuts, shelled | **20 g** | Afternoon snack; dry roasted, not fried |
| Vegetables | **450 g trimmed raw** | 150 g breakfast + 150 g lunch + 150 g dinner |
| Whole fruit | **120–150 g edible** | Banana, ripe papaya or guava; whole fruit rather than juice |
| Cooking oil | **20 ml total** | About four level 5 ml teaspoons, roughly 18 g; includes every curry and egg dish |
| Fish/chicken | Selected days below | About 80–110 g cooked edible portion, depending on actual yield |

Dry soy chunks are a food ingredient, distinct from whole soybeans and soybean oil. Choose a product whose label identifies soy protein and check its protein content; the estimate here assumes about 50 g protein per 100 g dry product. No particular imported brand is required.

180 g dry rice often yields roughly **450–600 g cooked rice**, depending on variety and water. Split the actual batch in half. The dry amount controls the allocation; cooked volume is only a visual guide. A thick bowl of dal and a watery bowl can contain very different amounts of protein.

### A normal day's meals

| When | Meal |
|---|---|
| Breakfast, after the short morning routine | 2 rotis from 80 g atta + 2 eggs + 150 g vegetables; tea/coffee if wanted |
| Lunch | Half the day's rice + dal made from 40 g dry lentils + 150 g vegetables + fish/chicken on scheduled days |
| Afternoon snack | 120–150 g edible fruit + 20 g peanuts; have it 45–90 minutes before training if comfortable, or afterwards |
| Dinner | Remaining rice + remaining 40 g dry equivalent of dal + curry from 40 g dry soy chunks + 150 g vegetables |
| Milk | 250 ml across the day, including anything used in tea/coffee |

On plant-only lunch days, the two eggs, daily dal, soy, milk, grains and peanuts still supply protein. Fish/chicken add variety and protein on selected days; they are not needed at every meal. If dinner is too bulky, move half the soy curry to lunch instead of leaving it uneaten.

The protein range is a practical budget compromise, not a guarantee of optimal muscle retention.

### Tea, coffee and water

Keep homemade tea/coffee. Plain tea/coffee adds little energy. Use milk from the **250 ml daily allowance**. Prefer no sugar, or keep the total added sugar to **at most two level teaspoons per day** across all cups, adding about 30 kcal. Honey, condensed milk and creamer also add energy and cost. Biscuits are a separate food, not part of the tea allowance.

Have caffeine early enough that it does not disturb sleep; avoid it in the afternoon/evening if it keeps you awake. Drink water regularly, with more in heat or when sweating; do not force a rigid amount regardless of thirst. Milk and tea also contribute fluid.

## 3. The weekly menu — repeat through November

Breakfast, snack, milk, daily dal and dinner soy stay as specified above. This table changes the lunch protein and vegetables only. Each listed vegetable serving is **150 g trimmed raw**, unless split between two vegetables.

| Day | Lunch addition | Breakfast vegetable | Lunch vegetable | Dinner vegetable |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sunday | Chicken jhol | Lau | Cabbage | Lal/kolmi/palang shak |
| Monday | Fish jhol | Radish | Green papaya | Pumpkin |
| Tuesday | No extra animal portion | Cabbage | Shak | Lau |
| Wednesday | Chicken jhol | Pumpkin | Cauliflower or cabbage | Shak |
| Thursday | Fish jhol | Green papaya | Radish | Cucumber, or cooked cabbage |
| Friday | No extra animal portion | Cabbage | Lau | 50 g carrot + 100 g cucumber, or cooked mixed vegetables |
| Saturday | No extra animal portion | Pumpkin | Radish or cabbage | Cabbage |

**November has 9 chicken lunches and 9 fish lunches.** Split the month's 2 kg purchased chicken into 9 similar portions, and the 2 kg fish into 9 portions: roughly **220 g purchase weight per meal**. Bone, head, skin and cooking losses mean that this may yield only about 80–110 g edible cooked flesh. Check actual yield once; do not mistake a 220 g bone-in piece for 220 g protein food.

For fish, choose affordable locally available fish. When practical, make **one of the two weekly fish meals a small-fish dish with safely edible softened bones**, such as mola, within the same fish allowance. Do not force down hard or sharp bones. Other fish meals can use pangash/tilapia or another affordable option.

The vegetable rotation is flexible: exchange equal **trimmed weights** within the basket when a particular item runs out, trims heavily, or costs more. It is not necessary to buy an expensive early cauliflower just to match Wednesday's name.

## 4. Which vegetables to buy in November

Aim to eat **13.5 kg trimmed vegetables over 30 days**. Budget **18 kg purchase weight**, allowing roughly 25% trimming overall. If your waste is greater, use edible leaves/stalks where suitable and buy more of the cheaper high-yield vegetables within the same allocation.

| Vegetable | Month, purchase weight | Role and cheaper alternative |
|---|---:|---|
| Cabbage / বাঁধাকপি | 3 kg | Main winter vegetable; use in place of expensive cauliflower |
| Cauliflower / ফুলকপি | 1.5 kg | Variety; replace with cabbage/lau if costly |
| Radish / মুলা | 2 kg | Cook root; clean edible leaves can join the shak allocation |
| Bottle gourd / লাউ | 3 kg | Jhol or mixed vegetable |
| Pumpkin / মিষ্টিকুমড়া | 2 kg | Orange vegetable; mix with dal or soy |
| Green papaya / কাঁচা পেঁপে | 2 kg | Cooked vegetable; distinct from ripe papaya fruit |
| Lal/kolmi/palang shak / লাল, কলমি, পালংশাক | 3 kg | Rotate affordable greens; weigh rather than assuming all bundles are equal |
| Cucumber / শসা | 1 kg | Wash well for salad, or replace with a cooked vegetable |
| Carrot / গাজর | 0.5 kg | Small variety allocation; replace with pumpkin if expensive |
| **Total** | **18 kg** | **Average vegetable allowance: ৳55/kg = ৳990** |

Buy greens in small batches two or three times weekly. Winter availability improves at different times by district; the above is a seasonal selection, not a guarantee of low prices on 1 November. Tomato, beans, brinjal or other local vegetables may replace items gram-for-gram if cheaper; they are not extra purchases.

Potato, sweet potato and green banana are starch exchanges. If wanted, replace about **30 g dry rice with 130–150 g peeled potato** rather than adding a large potato portion on top of the complete rice allowance. Keep the non-starchy vegetable portion too. Bitter gourd, lemon water and particular vegetables do not selectively burn belly fat.

### If actual prices exceed ৳6,000

1. Use the ৳179 reserve, then switch expensive early cauliflower/carrot/cucumber to cabbage, lau, radish or papaya. Preserve roughly the same trimmed vegetable weight. Choose the cheapest suitable fruit rather than dropping fruit automatically.
2. If still over, reduce chicken from **2 kg to 1 kg** and increase soy chunks from **1.2 to 1.4 kg** for the month. At the table's rates this saves **৳160 net**. Have chicken on five Sundays instead of Sunday + Wednesday, dividing the 1 kg across those five lunches; use the extra 200 g soy across the four Wednesday lunches, 50 g dry each, split between meals if preferred. This is a budget alternative, not an exact nutrient-equivalent exchange.
3. Example: soy at ৳350/kg and milk at ৳110/L add **৳135** to the original basket, giving **৳5,956** before further changes, still inside the cap. More expensive vegetables can require step 1 or 2 as well. Recalculate from the actual bill.
4. If these substitutions still cannot fit, the full basket is not feasible at those local prices. Record the shortfall for revision instead of claiming it costs ৳6,000 or replacing meals with tea. Keep eggs/dal/soy and sensible meal portions as priorities.

## 6. Cooking and nutrition details

Use jhol, steaming, boiling or lightly oiled covered cooking. Frying an egg is compatible with the plan when its oil is counted; deep-fried fish or vegetable dishes make the allowance much harder to follow. Split the daily 20 ml oil, for example **5 ml breakfast + 7.5 ml lunch + 7.5 ml dinner**. Water, onion and spices can make gravy without continually adding oil.

Build up legumes if you are not used to them: for days 1–3 start with 60 g dry dal and 20 g dry soy; days 4–7 use 80 g dal and 30 g soy; from day 8 use the full amounts if comfortable. During this introduction, add 20–30 g dry rice or one small extra roti if hungry; do not intentionally under-eat. Cook legumes thoroughly, spread them between meals, and slow the increase if bloating interferes with eating.

Calcium deserves attention on this budget: keep the milk, greens and edible-bone fish choices. Milk alone does not meet the adult calcium recommendation. Calcium content of greens, fish and soy products varies; this basket has not been verified to meet every micronutrient target. If those foods are routinely omitted, revise the plan rather than assuming rice/dal alone replace them.

Follow the food-safety notes in [COOKING-INSTRUCTIONS.md](COOKING-INSTRUCTIONS.md), particularly for cooked rice. Friday uses the same meals and portions; it is recovery from training, not a day to skip food or compensate for the week.

# November exercise — Saturday to Thursday, Friday recovery

**Start:** Sunday 1 November with the walking session. **Recovery Fridays:** 6, 13, 20, 27 November.
**Needs:** a wall, a sturdy counter or table fixed against a wall, a chair against a wall, a folded towel, a non-slip floor. No gym equipment.

## 1. Weekly schedule

| Day | Morning (10–12 min) | Afternoon (same slot daily, e.g. 5 PM; not right after a big meal) |
|---|---|---|
| Saturday | M | Strength A + 10 min brisk walk (about 35–40 min) |
| Sunday | M | Walking session (35–45 min) |
| Monday | M | Strength B + 10 min brisk walk |
| Tuesday | M | Walking session |
| Wednesday | M | Strength C + 10 min brisk walk |
| Thursday | M | Walking session |
| **Friday** | Rest | Rest, or an easy stroll plus the Friday mobility below |

**Brisk walking:** breathing is faster but you can still speak in sentences.
**Strength:** stop each set with about 2 reps left. Breathe out on the effort; never hold your breath.

### What each part is for

| Part | Physical benefit | Sexual-fitness benefit |
|---|---|---|
| Brisk walking, morning march | Burns calories, builds heart fitness and stamina. Belly fat falls through overall fat loss; no single exercise removes it | Better blood flow and stamina. Regular activity and weight loss are linked with better erectile function |
| Strength (legs, glutes, trunk) | Keeps muscle while you lose weight, protects the back | Hip power and endurance |
| Pelvic-floor set (section 3) | Bladder and core support | Strength and control of the muscles involved in erection and ejaculation |
| Hip mobility, relaxed breathing | Looser hips, less desk stiffness | Teaches the pelvic floor to relax; one that can only tighten works poorly |

## 2. M — morning routine

| Order | Movement | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Easy march in place, relaxed arms | 1 min |
| 2 | Brisk march in place, or brisk walk outside | 5 min |
| 3 | Standing hip-flexor stretch, 30 s each side; then supported deep-squat hold, 30 s (hold a door frame; folded towel under heels if they lift) | 1.5 min |
| 4 | Pelvic-floor set | 3–4 min |

Keep steps low and quiet. If 5 brisk minutes is too much at first, alternate 1 minute brisk, 1 minute easy. Hard squats, push-ups and planks stay in the afternoon.

## 3. Pelvic-floor set (Saturday–Thursday, lying on your back with knees bent, or seated)

**Find the muscles once:** while urinating, briefly slow the stream, or tighten as if holding back gas. The inward-and-up squeeze is the pelvic floor. Do not repeat this during urination; it is only for learning. Belly, thighs and buttocks stay relaxed.

1. **Relax breathing, 5 slow breaths:** belly and pelvic floor soften on the inhale; on the exhale, gently lift.
2. **Holds:** squeeze gently, hold, then relax fully for at least as long as you held.
3. **Quick flicks (from 15 November):** 10 fast squeeze-and-release.

| Dates | Holds | Flicks |
|---|---|---|
| 1–7 Nov | 8 × 3 seconds | none |
| 8–14 Nov | 10 × 5 seconds | none |
| 15–30 Nov | 10 × 5–8 seconds | 10 |

Breathe normally throughout. Do not bear down, and do not squeeze the glutes, abdomen or thighs instead.

**Stop and see a doctor or pelvic-floor physiotherapist** for pelvic, perineal or testicular pain or pressure, pain at ejaculation, or a weak or hesitant urine stream. These can mean the floor is already too tight, and more squeezing makes that worse. Persistent sexual difficulties need assessment, not more repetitions.

**Evidence:** pelvic-floor training has been studied mostly in men who already have erectile difficulty or early ejaculation. In a healthy man the benefit is plausible but not proven; done gently, the risk is low.

**Friday mobility (about 5 minutes, optional):** happy baby pose 45 s; cat-cow 10 slow reps; 5 relaxed breaths as in step 1. No squeezing on Friday.

## 4. Afternoon strength — about 35–40 minutes

Warm up 3 minutes: easy march, arm circles, a few practice squats. Rest 45–75 seconds between sets, longer if needed. Then do the sets, 10 minutes brisk walking, and 2 minutes easy walking.

|---|---|---|---|

| | Push-up (level below) | 1–2 × 5–6 | 2 × 6–10 |
| | Glute bridge | 1–2 × 8–10 | 2 × 10–15 |
| | Prone W raise | 1–2 × 6 | 2 × 6–10 |
| | Bird dog | 1 × 4–5 each side | 2 × 5–8 each side, 2 s pause |
| **B — Mon** | Supported reverse lunge | 1 × 5 each leg | 2 × 6–8 each leg |
| | Push-up | 1–2 × 5–6 | 2 × 6–10 |
| | Self-resisted row | 1 × 6 each arm | 2 × 6–8 each arm |
| | Dead bug, heel taps | 1 × 4–5 each side | 2 × 5–8 each side |
| | Glute bridge | 1–2 × 8–10 | 2 × 10–15 |
| **C — Wed** | Squat / sit-to-stand | 1–2 × 6–8 | 2 × 8–12 |

| | Prone W raise | 1–2 × 6 | 2 × 6–10 |
| | Side plank from knees | 1 × 8–10 s each side | 2 × 10–20 s each side |

**Push-up level:** use the hardest level where you can do the top of the rep range with clean form. Wall, then kitchen counter, then a lower sturdy surface that cannot slide (such as a stair), then knees on the floor, then full.

### Technique

| Movement | How | Easier |
|---|---|---|
| Sit-to-stand / squat | Hips back, knees follow toes, stand through the whole foot. Chair against a wall | Higher seat, shallower |
| Glute bridge | Knees bent, feet planted; squeeze buttocks and lift hips; stop before the lower back arches | Smaller lift |
| Prone W raise | Face down, forehead on a towel, elbows in a W; lift hands slightly, squeeze shoulder blades, pause 1–2 s | Standing shoulder-blade squeeze |
| Bird dog | Hands and knees; reach opposite arm and leg without twisting or sagging | Move only an arm or a leg |
| Reverse lunge | Hold a support, step back, bend only as far as comfortable | Sit-to-stand |
| Self-resisted row | Reach left arm forward, hold its wrist with the right hand; pull the left elbow back against gentle resistance; switch | Lighter resistance |
| Dead bug | On your back, knees above hips; lower one heel to tap the floor with the lower back steady; alternate | Slide one heel instead |
| Hip hinge | Soft knees, hips back with a long back, stand by bringing hips forward | Reach hips back to a wall |
| Side plank, knees | Elbow under shoulder, knees bent, lift hips in line | Shorter hold, or forearm against a wall |

Equipment-free pulling is limited; judge progress by controlled effort.

## 5. Walking sessions and progression

Every walking session: 5 minutes easy, the brisk block below, 5 minutes easy. Use a level route; indoors, march at the same effort.

| Dates | Brisk block (Sun, Tue, Thu) | Strength |
|---|---:|---|
| 1–7 Nov | 25 min | Week-1 amounts |
| 8–14 Nov | 30 min | Two sets if recovery is good |
| 15–21 Nov | 30 min | Add 1–2 reps within the ranges |
| 22–28 Nov | 30–35 min | Keep two sets; one progression only |
| 29–30 Nov | Sunday 30–35 min; Monday Strength B | Maintain, then review |

If the block is too long at first, start at a comfortable 30 minutes total and build. Once every set reaches the top of its range for two sessions, change **one** thing: a lower push-up level, a 3-second squat lowering, or 5 more seconds on the side plank (maximum 25 s). No maximal tests or sprints this month.

## 6. Recovery, safety, check-in

- Sleep 7–9 hours. During long study sessions, stand and move for 2 minutes every hour.
- Mild soreness is normal. Sharp pain, joint pain or back pain that radiates: stop that exercise. Chest pain, fainting or unusual severe breathlessness: stop and get urgent help.
- If you miss a session, resume at the next scheduled one. Never double up or train on Friday to make up for it.
- **Check-in every two weeks:** weight and waist at navel level, on waking, same conditions. About 0.5 kg per week is a healthy pace. If the trend stalls for three weeks, add 10 minutes to a walking day before cutting food.
- **Waist target:** under 90 cm. For South Asian men, health risk from belly fat rises above about 90 cm. The long-term weight goal is 75 kg; this month promises no particular number. Section 7 covers the path after November.

## 7. After November — the path to the wedding

Fat loss comes first, so you are physically and sexually fit well before the wedding. The year after that is for holding the weight and getting stronger. Weight loss slows as you get lighter, so treat these as the earliest dates, not deadlines.

| Period | Rough target | Training |
|---|---|---|
| Dec 2026 – Feb 2027 | About 90 kg by the end of February | Walking sessions 40–45 min. Strength moves to 3 sets once 2 sets feel easy. Lower the push-up level one step at a time |
| Mar – Jun 2027 | About 82 kg; waist under 90 cm | Brisk walking 45–60 min most days. Add resistance bands (row, band squat). Floor push-ups, single-leg sit-to-stand. Once you are near 88 kg, add jog intervals: 1 min jog, 2 min walk, 5–8 rounds |
| Jul – Nov 2027 | 75–78 kg | Jog intervals lengthen. Strength 3 days, harder each month. Re-calculate the diet at about every 8 kg lost |
| Dec 2027 onward | Hold your weight; no further loss needed | Keep strength progressing; keep walking and the pelvic-floor set about 3 days a week |

Typical pattern, not a guarantee: stamina and energy often improve within 2–3 months, and most sexual-fitness gains follow the weight loss. Keep sleeping 7–9 hours; short sleep raises hunger and can lower testosterone.

## 8. Foods that support the goals

No single food boosts sexual performance. Diets rich in fish, vegetables, fruit and legumes are linked with better erectile function, and your cooking guide already follows that pattern. Within the budget, swap rather than add:

- **Small fish** such as mola or tengra, once or twice a week, in place of part of the chicken or pangas. Check the local price before swapping.
- **Greens daily** (lalshak, palong) and **guava** or watermelon in season.
- **Protein at every meal** (eggs, dal, milk, fish) rather than one large meal.
- Keep sugary drinks, mishti and fried snacks out. Drink water through the day.

## 9. Supplements and baseline checks

**Nothing here is required.** Food comes first. Buy from a reputable pharmacy or seller, because counterfeit supplements are common. Start one at a time, and stop if your stomach is upset. If you take any medicine or have a health condition, ask a doctor first.

| Supplement | Why | Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vitamin D3 | Low vitamin D is common in Bangladesh despite the sun | Blood test first. Without one, 1,000 IU/day is generally within safe limits; do not exceed 4,000 IU/day without a doctor | Take with a meal that contains some fat |
| Basic multivitamin-mineral | Insurance while your diet has few vegetables | One tablet a day, about 100% of daily values, no megadoses | Can stop if blood tests are fine and your diet widens |
| Creatine monohydrate | The best-studied sports supplement; helps strength training | 3–5 g/day, any time, with water | Start in month 2–3, when training gets harder. Expect 1–2 kg of water weight on the scale, not fat. Not for people with kidney disease |
| Fish oil (omega-3) | Only if you cannot eat small fish 1–2 times a week | About 250–500 mg EPA+DHA per day | Optional |
| Whey protein | Only if food cannot cover your protein | One scoop a day | Costly; check authenticity |

**Skip:** testosterone boosters, tribulus, maca, ginseng, ashwagandha, L-arginine or L-citrulline pills, high-dose zinc (excess zinc causes copper deficiency; get zinc from food), fat burners and stimulant pre-workouts. Take iron or extra B12 only if a test shows you are low.

**One-time baseline checks in the first month:** blood pressure, fasting glucose or HbA1c, a lipid profile and vitamin D (25-OH). South Asians develop type 2 diabetes at lower weights, so this is worth doing at 98 kg. Test testosterone or thyroid only if you have symptoms such as low desire, persistent fatigue or erection difficulty. See a doctor early if erection or ejaculation difficulty persists; many causes are easy to treat.

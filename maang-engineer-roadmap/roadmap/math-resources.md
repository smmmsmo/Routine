# Mathematics Resources

Use **one primary resource per subject**, not five simultaneously. Add a second source only when the first explanation does not click or when more problems are needed.

## Foundation repair

### Primary

- **Khan Academy:** Arithmetic, Pre-algebra, Algebra 1, Algebra 2, Geometry, Trigonometry, Precalculus.
- **OpenStax:** *Algebra and Trigonometry* — free textbook with exercises.

### Extra practice

- *The Art of Problem Solving, Volume 1* — strong for algebra, counting, number theory, and problem-solving habits.
- AoPS Alcumus — adaptive problem practice.

## Proofs and discrete mathematics

### Primary

- Richard Hammack, *Book of Proof* — free, friendly introduction to proof writing.
- Oscar Levin, *Discrete Mathematics: An Open Introduction* — free and suitable for CS.

### Deeper/reference

- Kenneth Rosen, *Discrete Mathematics and Its Applications*.
- Graham, Knuth, and Patashnik, *Concrete Mathematics* — use selectively after the basics.

### Competitive/problem-solving

- CSES Mathematics section.
- Codeforces problem tags: math, combinatorics, number theory, probabilities.
- CP-Algorithms articles for implementation-focused number theory and combinatorics.
- AoPS forums and problem sets for non-routine mathematics.

## Calculus

### Primary options

Choose one textbook/course pair:

- James Stewart, *Calculus*; or
- OpenStax, *Calculus Volumes 1–3* — free; or
- MIT OpenCourseWare 18.01 Single Variable Calculus and 18.02 Multivariable Calculus.

### Intuition

- 3Blue1Brown, *Essence of Calculus*.
- Paul's Online Math Notes for concise explanations and practice.

### Problem practice

- textbook exercises, including conceptual and applied questions;
- MIT OCW assignments and exams;
- selected challenging problems after standard skills become reliable.

## Linear algebra

### Recommended sequence

1. 3Blue1Brown, *Essence of Linear Algebra* — intuition.
2. Gilbert Strang, MIT OCW 18.06 — full course.
3. Strang, *Introduction to Linear Algebra* — primary textbook.
4. NumPy notebooks for implementation and visualization.

### Additional reference

- Jim Hefferon, *Linear Algebra* — free textbook.
- Sheldon Axler, *Linear Algebra Done Right* — later, for a more theoretical treatment.

## Probability and statistics

### Primary probability

- Harvard Stat 110 lectures and Blitzstein & Hwang, *Introduction to Probability*.
- MIT OCW 6.041/6.431 probabilistic systems materials.

### Accessible alternatives

- Khan Academy Statistics and Probability for repair/review.
- OpenIntro Statistics — free introductory statistics textbook.

### ML-focused bridge

- Deisenroth, Faisal, and Ong, *Mathematics for Machine Learning* — use after basic calculus, linear algebra, and probability.
- Murphy, *Probabilistic Machine Learning: An Introduction* — selected chapters later.

## Optimization

### Primary introduction

- Boyd and Vandenberghe, *Convex Optimization* — free online; study selected introductory chapters.
- Stanford EE364a materials for convex optimization.

### Practical ML optimization

- *Dive into Deep Learning* optimization chapters.
- Goodfellow, Bengio, and Courville, *Deep Learning*, optimization chapters.

Do not begin with advanced convex optimization before gradients, linear algebra, and basic probability are comfortable.

## AI and research mathematics integration

### Bridge resources

- Deisenroth et al., *Mathematics for Machine Learning*.
- Goodfellow et al., *Deep Learning*, especially linear algebra, probability, numerical computation, and optimization chapters.
- Stanford CS229 notes for derivations in classical machine learning.
- *Dive into Deep Learning* for executable explanations.

### Research-reading habits

- Papers with Code for implementations and benchmarks.
- arXiv papers only after finding a guided course, survey, or textbook chapter on the topic.
- Semantic Scholar or Google Scholar for citation trails and related work.

A paper is not a course. Use papers to extend a foundation, not replace one.

## Mathematical computing

### Tools

- Python basics.
- NumPy for vectors, matrices, random simulation, and numerical work.
- Matplotlib for visualization.
- SymPy for symbolic checks, not as a replacement for derivation.
- Jupyter notebooks for experiments and written explanations.

### Practice projects

- visualize function transformations and Taylor approximations;
- simulate the law of large numbers and central limit theorem;
- implement Gaussian elimination;
- implement least squares and PCA;
- implement gradient descent;
- implement linear and logistic regression from scratch;
- derive and code backpropagation for a tiny neural network.

## How to choose exercises

For each chapter, aim for:

- 5–10 basic mechanical questions;
- 5 intermediate mixed questions;
- 2–5 conceptual or proof questions;
- 1–3 difficult unfamiliar problems;
- one written summary or small implementation.

Skip redundant easy exercises once accuracy is consistently high. Do not skip difficult problems merely because the solution is not immediate.

## Resource discipline

- Finish a defined section before switching courses.
- Track chapters completed and problems solved.
- Keep a mistake log.
- Re-solve representative problems after 1 day, 1 week, and 1 month.
- Use videos for explanation, books for precision, and exercises for mastery.
- Never confuse recognizing a solution with being able to produce it.

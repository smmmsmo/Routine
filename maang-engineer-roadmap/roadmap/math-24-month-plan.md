# Two-Year Mathematics Roadmap for AI, Research, and Problem Solving

This plan starts from rusty school mathematics and builds toward the mathematical maturity needed to read introductory AI/ML papers, understand derivations, and keep enjoying competitive-mathematics-style problem solving.

## Outcome after 24 months

By the end, I should be able to:

- manipulate algebra, logarithms, exponents, trigonometry, and inequalities confidently;
- write and understand basic mathematical proofs;
- use combinatorics, number theory, recurrence relations, and graph ideas in algorithms;
- understand single-variable and multivariable calculus;
- understand vectors, matrices, eigenvalues, projections, and least squares;
- work with probability distributions, expectation, variance, conditional probability, and Bayes' theorem;
- understand optimization, gradients, convexity, and gradient-based learning;
- follow the mathematics in introductory machine-learning courses and many applied papers;
- implement important ideas numerically in Python/NumPy;
- explain solutions clearly instead of only memorizing formulas.

## Study rule

For every topic:

1. Learn the definitions and intuition.
2. Derive the central formulas when possible.
3. Solve routine exercises until the mechanics are comfortable.
4. Solve several unfamiliar problems.
5. Explain the topic from memory.
6. Implement or visualize it when useful.
7. Revisit it through spaced review.

A topic is not complete merely because a video playlist ended.

# Parallel recreational problem-solving track

This track supports competitive programming but should not consume the AI/research foundation.

## Weekly menu

Choose one or two areas:

- number theory;
- combinatorics;
- inequalities;
- algebraic manipulation;
- recurrences;
- graph theory;
- probability;
- geometry;
- invariants and constructive reasoning.

## Problem-solving process

1. Work unaided for 30–60 minutes.
2. Record observations, failed approaches, and examples.
3. Read only a hint when stuck.
4. Finish the solution yourself where possible.
5. Read an editorial or official solution.
6. Write the key idea in an error log.
7. Re-solve the problem after one week and one month.

## Target

- normal month: 8–12 non-routine mathematics problems;
- busy month: 4 carefully reviewed problems;
- never chase counts at the expense of understanding.

# What is deliberately postponed

These are valuable, but not required before the core roadmap is complete:

- real analysis at full theorem-proof depth;
- measure-theoretic probability;
- abstract algebra;
- topology;
- differential geometry;
- advanced functional analysis;
- stochastic calculus;
- advanced convex analysis;
- partial differential equations.

After Month 24, choose based on research direction. For theoretical ML, probability, or advanced research, the natural next step is real analysis, rigorous probability, and deeper optimization.

# AI/ML Research Skills

## Reading Papers

Use a three-pass method:

1. **Orientation:** title, abstract, figures, conclusion, and claimed contribution.
2. **Understanding:** problem, assumptions, method, datasets, baselines, metrics, and main results.
3. **Verification:** equations, implementation details, ablations, limitations, citations, and reproducibility.

For every paper, record:

- What problem is being solved?
- Why do existing methods fall short?
- What is genuinely new?
- What evidence supports the claims?
- Are the baselines and comparisons fair?
- What assumptions or limitations matter?
- What experiment would most strongly challenge the conclusion?

## Literature Review

- Search by problem, method, dataset, benchmark, and citation graph
- Distinguish foundational papers, surveys, replications, and recent extensions
- Maintain a concept map and annotated bibliography
- Track contradictory evidence rather than only confirming papers
- Prefer primary sources for technical claims

## Experimental Design

- State a falsifiable hypothesis
- Establish simple and strong baselines
- Control data splits, random seeds, compute budgets, and evaluation procedures
- Run ablations that isolate important components
- Report uncertainty and repeated trials where appropriate
- Avoid test-set tuning and selective reporting
- Record negative results and failed attempts

## Reproduction Workflow

1. Read the paper and inspect official code when available.
2. Reconstruct the data and evaluation protocol.
3. Reproduce a baseline before the proposed method.
4. Match one headline result or a meaningful subset.
5. Investigate gaps systematically.
6. Document environmental and implementation differences.
7. Publish code, logs, results, and limitations clearly.

## Scientific Writing

- Problem and motivation
- Related work
- Method with notation and assumptions
- Experimental setup
- Results with uncertainty
- Error analysis and ablations
- Limitations, ethics, and reproducibility
- Clear figures, captions, tables, and citations

## Yearly Research Targets

- Read at least 20 papers carefully
- Write concise summaries for at least 12
- Present or teach at least 4 papers
- Reproduce at least 1 paper or major experiment
- Write 1 substantial technical report

## Mastery Checklist

- [ ] I distinguish claims from evidence.
- [ ] I can identify weak baselines or confounded experiments.
- [ ] I report uncertainty and negative results.
- [ ] I can reproduce a method from a paper and codebase.
- [ ] I write clear methods, results, and limitations.
- [ ] I can propose a meaningful follow-up experiment.

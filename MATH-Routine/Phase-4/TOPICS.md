# Phase 4 — Complete Graduate Mathematics Topic and Subtopic List

**Scope:** 64 weeks, 1,344 hours, following [SCHEDULE.md](SCHEDULE.md). **Core** below means a required selected graduate introduction with proof/problem competence, **not** mastery of an entire research subfield.

## A. Measure Theory and Lebesgue Integration — Weeks 1–10 (210 h)

### A1 — Sigma-algebras and measures
- [ ] Sigma-algebras, Borel sigma-algebra, countable operations and measurable spaces.
- [ ] Measures, probability measures, counting/Dirac/Lebesgue examples.
- [ ] Outer measures, Carathéodory measurability and construction **selected proof**.
- [ ] Lebesgue measure on real line and Euclidean spaces; measurable sets.
- [ ] Measure continuity from above/below, null sets and almost-everywhere properties.

### A2 — Integration and convergence
- [ ] Simple measurable functions and Lebesgue integral construction.
- [ ] Monotone convergence theorem, Fatou's lemma and dominated convergence theorem: precise assumptions and proofs or proof reconstruction.
- [ ] Integrable functions, comparison to Riemann integral, counterexamples.
- [ ] Modes of convergence: almost everywhere, in measure and in L^p; counterexamples.
- [ ] Egorov's theorem, Lusin's theorem **selected statements/examples**.

### A3 — Product and signed measures
- [ ] Product measures, Tonelli and Fubini theorem, iterated integrals.
- [ ] Signed measures, Hahn and Jordan decompositions **intro**.
- [ ] Absolute continuity and Radon–Nikodym theorem; densities and conditional-expectation link.
- [ ] Change of variables and elementary differentiation of measures **selected**.

### A4 — Lp foundations
- [ ] Hölder and Minkowski inequalities with proofs.
- [ ] Lp normed spaces, equivalence classes almost everywhere and completeness **proof outline**.
- [ ] Dense subsets, approximation by simple and smooth functions **selected**.
- [ ] Basic duality interpretation and use in probability/functional analysis.

## B. Functional Analysis — Weeks 11–18 (168 h)

- [ ] Normed linear spaces, Banach spaces, continuous linear maps and operator norms.
- [ ] Bounded versus continuous linear operators; dual spaces and standard examples.
- [ ] Hahn–Banach extension and geometric separation: theorem statements and selected proof.
- [ ] Uniform boundedness principle, open mapping theorem and closed graph theorem.
- [ ] Weak and weak-* topologies; weak convergence and elementary duality.
- [ ] Hilbert spaces: inner products, orthogonal projection, Riesz representation.
- [ ] Orthonormal systems, Parseval/Bessel inequalities, Hilbert bases.
- [ ] Compact operators, spectrum/resolvent, adjoints and spectral theorem for compact self-adjoint operators.
- [ ] Bounded self-adjoint operator intuition and infinite-dimensional eigenproblem limitations.
- [ ] Connections to RKHS, least squares, integral operators and inverse problems **selected**.

## C. Advanced Probability and Martingales — Weeks 19–27 (189 h)

- [ ] Probability triple (Ω,F,P) and random variables as measurable maps.
- [ ] Distribution/pushforward measures; probability integral and expectations.
- [ ] Independence of events, sigma-algebras and random variables.
- [ ] Modes of stochastic convergence: a.s., probability, Lp, distribution.
- [ ] Basic implications and counterexamples among convergence types.
- [ ] Borel–Cantelli lemmas and applications.
- [ ] Laws of large numbers **rigorous selected proofs**, characteristic functions, weak convergence.
- [ ] Central limit theorem proof route **selected**, Lévy continuity theorem statement and use.
- [ ] Conditional expectation via Radon–Nikodym/orthogonal projection, its properties.
- [ ] Filtrations, stopping times, adapted processes.
- [ ] Discrete-time martingales, sub/supermartingales, tower property.
- [ ] Doob's maximal inequality, optional stopping with precise sufficient conditions.
- [ ] Martingale convergence theorem **selected statement/proof**; uniform integrability **intro**.
- [ ] Markov processes, transition kernels, ergodic intuition **selected**.

## D. Stochastic Processes and Calculus — Weeks 28–35 (168 h)

- [ ] Markov chains, transition semigroups and stationary distributions **advanced selected review**.
- [ ] Poisson process, exponential interarrival times and counting-process properties.
- [ ] Brownian motion: finite-dimensional distributions, paths and quadratic variation.
- [ ] Independent increments, scaling and reflection principle **selected**.
- [ ] Stochastic integrals for adapted square-integrable integrands: simple integrands → L2 completion.
- [ ] Itô isometry and Itô formula with assumptions.
- [ ] Stochastic differential equations (SDEs), interpretation of drift and diffusion.
- [ ] Existence/uniqueness under Lipschitz/linear-growth conditions **theorem and proof outline**.
- [ ] Geometric Brownian motion and Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process exact solutions.
- [ ] Stochastic numerical schemes: Euler–Maruyama, strong/weak error **intro**.
- [ ] Stochastic processes and diffusion/score modeling links **intro**.
- [ ] Fokker–Planck/Kolmogorov equations **intro**, stochastic control and Girsanov **exposure only**, not a full theorem course.

## E. Advanced Convex and Numerical Optimization — Weeks 36–43 (168 h)

- [ ] Convex analysis: epigraphs, closed/proper convex functions, subgradients.
- [ ] Fenchel conjugate, Fenchel–Young inequality, biconjugation **selected**.
- [ ] Separating hyperplanes, duality and saddle-point formulations.
- [ ] Strong convexity, L-smoothness, cocoercivity **selected**.
- [ ] First-order complexity bounds, smooth/strongly convex convergence rates.
- [ ] Nesterov acceleration and momentum; proof of a selected accelerated rate.
- [ ] Proximal maps, Moreau envelope, forward–backward splitting and FISTA **selected**.
- [ ] Mirror descent, Bregman divergence and simplex/entropy geometry.
- [ ] Primal–dual and alternating-direction methods (ADMM) **mathematical formulation/intro**.
- [ ] Interior point/log-barrier and Newton method **selected**.
- [ ] Stochastic optimization, variance and regularization; online learning connections.
- [ ] Numerical conditioning and limits of guarantees in nonconvex ML.
- [ ] Optimal transport: couplings, Wasserstein distance and Kantorovich primal/dual **intro**.

## F. Smooth Manifolds and Differential Geometry — Weeks 44–50 (147 h)

- [ ] Prerequisite review: topology, charts on sphere, torus and Euclidean spaces.
- [ ] Smooth manifolds, atlases, coordinate transitions and smooth maps.
- [ ] Tangent spaces, tangent/cotangent vectors and differential/pushforward.
- [ ] Vector fields, integral curves, Lie bracket and flows **selected**.
- [ ] Submersions, immersions, regular-value theorem and submanifolds **selected**.
- [ ] Tensor fields, differential forms, exterior derivative.
- [ ] Orientation, integration on manifolds, generalized Stokes theorem **basic examples**.
- [ ] Riemannian metrics, curve length, geodesics and Levi–Civita connection **selected intro**.
- [ ] Curvature intuition, basic geometric optimization/manifold learning connections.
- [ ] Full Sard/transversality, de Rham cohomology and Riemannian comparison theorems **deferred**.

## G. Sobolev Spaces, Weak PDEs and Variational Methods — Weeks 51–57 (147 h)

- [ ] Classical PDE recap: elliptic/parabolic/hyperbolic classification.
- [ ] Distribution/generalized functions, test functions, weak derivatives.
- [ ] Sobolev spaces W^{k,p}, H^1, weak versus strong solutions.
- [ ] Density/approximation and trace results **statements and selected examples**.
- [ ] Poincaré inequality, Sobolev embeddings **selected**.
- [ ] Variational formulation of Poisson/elliptic boundary-value problems.
- [ ] Bounded/coercive bilinear forms and Lax–Milgram theorem.
- [ ] Weak existence/uniqueness for model elliptic equations.
- [ ] Energy estimates, weak formulations for heat/wave equations **selected**.
- [ ] Finite-element/Galerkin approximation connection **intro**, not a full FEM course.
- [ ] Classical existence/regularity deep theory and nonlinear PDE research **deferred**.

## H. Selected Harmonic and Fourier Analysis — Weeks 58–61 (84 h)

- [ ] Fourier transform on R and R^d, inversion and Schwartz functions **selected**.
- [ ] Convolution, approximate identities, Fourier multipliers **elementary**.
- [ ] Plancherel/Parseval theorem and connection to L2 Hilbert spaces.
- [ ] Fourier series convergence, Fejér summation and pointwise/uniform issues **selected**.
- [ ] Fourier transforms as PDE solvers and smoothing.
- [ ] Tempered distributions and transform **brief introduction**.
- [ ] Sampling, wavelets or time-frequency localization **one selected example**.
- [ ] Full Calderón–Zygmund theory and advanced singular integrals **deferred**.

## I. Graduate Integration, Reading and Final Review — Weeks 62–64 (63 h)

- [ ] Reconstruct selected core proofs in measure theory and functional analysis.
- [ ] Derive conditional expectation/martingale facts and one stochastic-integral calculation.
- [ ] Solve mixed optimization, geometry, PDE and Fourier proof problems.
- [ ] Read 2–3 appropriately scoped graduate/research expository papers with complete notation maps.
- [ ] Document theorem assumptions, examples/counterexamples and unresolved questions.
- [ ] Produce a concise research-specialty shortlist for Phase 5.
- [ ] Fresh, unseen cumulative qualifying assessment; target 80% with no critical topic gap.

## What “complete Phase 4” means

It means study **all the named topics at the depth stated above** with selected original proof work, exercises and comprehension. It does **not** mean finishing all of Axler, Brezis, Williams, Durrett, Øksendal, Nesterov, Lee, Evans or Stein–Shakarchi cover to cover in 64 weeks. Graduate mathematics becomes research-level only through specialization.

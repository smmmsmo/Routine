## Primary-book selection logic

- **Axler** fits first-year graduate measure theory and has a *legal open-access* edition. Use the university's resources if printed text is preferable; free is not a negative quality signal.
- **Brezis** intentionally serves two connected blocks: functional analysis and weak PDEs. The shared text keeps definitions consistent across prerequisites.
- **Williams** puts discrete-time martingales at the center of rigorous probability, a valuable bridge to stochastic analysis.
- **Øksendal** is a readable graduate introduction to Itô calculus and SDEs without treating all general semimartingale theory.
- **Nesterov** is demanding and useful for proof-based optimization convergence rates, with Bubeck providing a different presentation and modern algorithmic breadth.
- **Lee** is rigorous, but a *seven-week selection*, not a claim to graduate-level specialization in geometry. Formal full treatment of topological manifolds is not included.
- **Stein–Shakarchi** is a serious introduction to Fourier analysis. Only selected topics fit in four weeks.

## 21-hour weekly reading and exercise budget

- **6 h** — carefully chosen textbook sections, occasional authoritative lectures.
- **10 h** — independent theorem proofs, problems, counterexamples, calculations.
- **2 h** — optional experiment (SDE paths, optimizers, spectral/PDE numerical test) or one deep proof.
- **3 h** — closed-book spaced review and cumulative weekly assessment.
- **Total 21 h/week**, entirely inside the 1,344-hour phase. Do not add “homework beyond the plan”.

## How to use paid vs free materials

Your university may provide licenses for Springer, Cambridge, Wiley or AMS texts; request the exact title/edition above. No specific paid purchase is mandatory when a strong authorized open-access edition exists. Revisit the links and edition availability when you reach Phase 4.

## Scope honesty

At 1,344 hours you can aim to **study all explicitly listed selected graduate gateways with proof exercises**. You cannot deeply cover *every chapter* of nine substantial graduate textbooks, or guarantee PhD-level mastery across all eight areas. Phase 5 is reserved for **deep specialization**.

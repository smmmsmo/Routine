# Phase 1 — Core University Mathematics

**Status:** Fully designed in advance. **Actual study order:** Phase 0 → **Phase 1** → Phase 2 → Phase 3 → Phase 4 → Phase 5. Phase 1 starts after the Phase 0 exit gate, not automatically on a date.

## 1. Full timeframe

- **64 weeks × 21 hours/week = 1,344 scheduled study hours.**
- **3 hours/day × 7 days/week = 448 scheduled study days.**
- **About 14.7 calendar months** if every day is studied at the planned intensity.
- One week's **21 hours includes all reading, exercises, proofs, programming and review**; no hidden homework hours.
- Phase 0 is revision/readiness; Phase 1 is the first **complete university-level learning pass**. Forgetting school logarithms or trigonometry must be repaired before this phase begins.

## Entry: Phase 0 must be genuinely complete

- Algebra, exponent laws and **logarithms** without memorization-only errors.
- Unit circle, **trigonometry**, function graphs, equations and inequalities.
- Limit/derivative intuition; introductory set/proof vocabulary.
- Phase 0 gate and retention recheck passed.

## Why this order?

- **Proof literacy first** prepares you to explain and verify every subsequent mathematics topic.
- **Single-variable calculus** builds the analytic foundation for multivariable calculus, ODEs, probability and analysis.
- **Linear algebra before the multivariable block** makes Jacobians, linear approximations, eigenstructure and future optimization easier.
- **Discrete mathematics near the end** expands earlier logic and counting into the graph/algorithm foundations needed in Phase 2 and theoretical CS.
- The final 3 weeks are reserved for cumulative coverage rather than pretending every topic fits with no review.

## Weekly time allocation

A typical 21-hour week:
- **6 hours:** one primary book + targeted course lectures.
- **10 hours:** independent exercises, derivations and proofs.
- **2 hours:** numerical/algorithmic implementations or difficult unseen problems.
- **3 hours:** cumulative revision + closed-book assessment.
- **Total: 21 hours**, not 24.

## Phase 1 completion

- All named [topics](TOPICS.md) studied at assigned depth.
- Complete representative textbook/university problem sets and independently justify main theorems/derivations.
- Cumulative exam **≥80%**, with no critical deficiency in calculus, linear algebra, logic/proofs or discrete reasoning.
- Move to [Phase 2](../Phase-2/README.md) only when ready. The **64 weeks are a pacing target**, not a guarantee of mathematical mastery; extend if necessary.

## Scope honesty

The entire subject of university mathematics does not fit in 1,344 hours. The plan completes a strong *first pass* through the explicitly listed calculus, linear algebra and discrete math foundation. Deep epsilon–delta analysis, advanced abstract algebra, probability, statistics, differential equations, numerical analysis, optimization and graduate mathematics are scheduled in subsequent phases.

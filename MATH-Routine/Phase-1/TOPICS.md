# Phase 1 — Complete University Foundation Topic List

**Time budget:** 64 weeks / 1,344 hours. Complete every item here at the stated first-university-course depth, using [SCHEDULE.md](SCHEDULE.md) and [RESOURCES.md](RESOURCES.md). Advanced topics named **intro** are previews, not claims of graduate-level mastery.

## A. Mathematical Logic and Proof Writing — Weeks 1–6 (126 h)

### A1 — Language, logic and sets
- [ ] Mathematical statements vs open sentences; variables, domains and precise notation.
- [ ] Propositions: negation, conjunction, disjunction, implication and biconditional.
- [ ] Truth tables, logical equivalences, De Morgan laws, contrapositive versus converse.
- [ ] Predicates, universal/existential quantifiers, nested quantifiers and their negations.
- [ ] Sets, subsets, equality, empty/power sets, unions/intersections/differences/complements.
- [ ] Cartesian products; indexed set operations; Venn diagrams as examples, **not** proofs.

### A2 — Functions and relations rigorously
- [ ] Relations, equivalence relations, equivalence classes, partitions.
- [ ] Partial orders, Hasse diagrams and examples from divisibility/subsets.
- [ ] Functions as maps: domain/codomain/image/preimage.
- [ ] Injective, surjective and bijective maps; inverses and compositions.
- [ ] Countable and uncountable sets **introductory**; Cantor diagonal argument.

### A3 — Proof toolbox
- [ ] Definitions → theorem → proof; necessity/sufficiency and logical scope.
- [ ] Direct proof, proof by cases, proof by contrapositive and contradiction.
- [ ] Counterexamples, existence, uniqueness and equivalence proofs.
- [ ] Mathematical induction, strong induction, minimal-counterexample reasoning.
- [ ] Summation and divisibility induction proofs; loop-invariant intuition.
- [ ] Write complete proof sentences, check assumptions, find circular reasoning.

### A4 — Proof fluency
- [ ] Prove sum/product/divisibility and set-identity statements.
- [ ] Prove basic injectivity/surjectivity and equivalence-class facts.
- [ ] Construct counterexamples to false converses.
- [ ] Critique flawed proofs and rewrite correctly.

## B. Calculus I — Weeks 7–17 (231 h)

### B1 — Limits and continuity
- [ ] Mathematical models, functions and graphical/numerical/algebraic viewpoints.
- [ ] Secant slopes, tangent slope and limit definition.
- [ ] Limit laws; one-sided limits; infinity and infinite limits.
- [ ] Factorization/rationalization, squeeze theorem, trigonometric limits.
- [ ] Continuity at points and intervals; removable/jump/infinite discontinuities.
- [ ] Intermediate Value Theorem and continuous-function applications.
- [ ] Epsilon–delta definition **working introduction** and elementary proofs; full rigorous calculus deferred to Phase 2 analysis.

### B2 — Differentiation
- [ ] Derivative from difference quotient; differentiability vs continuity.
- [ ] Derivative rules: constant, power, sum, product and quotient.
- [ ] Chain rule and composite functions; implicit differentiation.
- [ ] Trig, inverse-trig, exponential and logarithmic derivatives.
- [ ] Inverse-function and logarithmic differentiation.
- [ ] Higher derivatives and meaning of second derivative.
- [ ] Linear approximation, differentials and error estimates.
- [ ] Related rates and interpretation of units.

### B3 — Mean Value and applications
- [ ] Rolle and Mean Value theorems: geometric meaning and selected proofs.
- [ ] Critical points, Fermat theorem, increasing/decreasing tests.
- [ ] Concavity, inflection, first/second derivative tests.
- [ ] Optimization with word models and constraints.
- [ ] Curve sketching, end behavior and asymptotes.
- [ ] L'Hôpital's rule: valid hypotheses, indeterminate forms and alternatives.
- [ ] Newton's method for root finding, numerical convergence intuition.
- [ ] Taylor polynomials and remainder/error intuition.

## C. Calculus II — Weeks 18–28 (231 h)

### C1 — Integration and the Fundamental Theorem
- [ ] Antiderivatives, definite integrals, signed area and Riemann sums.
- [ ] Basic Riemann integrability intuition and Fundamental Theorem of Calculus.
- [ ] Substitution and changing bounds.
- [ ] Integration by parts, trigonometric integrals and trig substitution.
- [ ] Partial fractions and rational functions.
- [ ] Improper integrals and convergence.
- [ ] Numerical quadrature: midpoint, trapezoid, Simpson with error intuition.

### C2 — Applications and modeling
- [ ] Area between curves, volumes by slicing/disks/washers/shells.
- [ ] Arc length and surface area of revolution (where formula applies).
- [ ] Work, average value, center of mass and physical accumulation models.
- [ ] Separable differential equations **brief application only**; full ODE course Phase 2.
- [ ] Parametric curves, derivatives and integrals of parametric trajectories.
- [ ] Polar coordinates, slopes and areas.

### C3 — Sequences, series and approximation
- [ ] Sequences and convergence; monotonic/bounded sequences **calculus level**.
- [ ] Series and partial sums; geometric and telescoping series.
- [ ] Divergence test, integral and comparison tests.
- [ ] Alternating series and absolute versus conditional convergence.
- [ ] Ratio and root tests; when tests are inconclusive.
- [ ] Power series, convergence intervals and radii.
- [ ] Termwise differentiation/integration of power series (calculus treatment).
- [ ] Taylor/Maclaurin series and Taylor error estimates.
- [ ] Standard expansions for exp, trig and log on valid domains.

## D. Linear Algebra I + Proof-Based Foundations — Weeks 29–40 (252 h)

### D1 — Matrix methods and systems
- [ ] Vectors, matrices, dimensions/shapes, addition, multiplication and transposition.
- [ ] Systems of linear equations; Gaussian elimination, row operations and pivots.
- [ ] Row echelon/RREF, rank, free variables, consistency and solution geometry.
- [ ] Matrix inverses and factorization **LU introduction**.
- [ ] Determinant properties, geometric volume, invertibility links.
- [ ] Matrix-vector products as linear transformations.

### D2 — Vector spaces and linear maps
- [ ] Subspaces, span, independence, basis and dimension.
- [ ] Column space, row space, null space, left null space.
- [ ] Rank–nullity theorem and proof.
- [ ] Linear maps, kernels, images and injective/surjective equivalences.
- [ ] Matrix of a linear transformation, coordinate systems/change of basis.
- [ ] Polynomial and function vector spaces **elementary examples**.
- [ ] Linear isomorphism, similarity and coordinate invariance.

### D3 — Inner products, eigenstructure and factorization
- [ ] Dot products, norms, angles, orthogonal subspaces and complements.
- [ ] Orthogonal projection and projection matrices.
- [ ] Gram–Schmidt, orthonormal bases and QR factorization **introduction**.
- [ ] Least squares and normal equations; why they work.
- [ ] Eigenvalues, eigenvectors and characteristic polynomial.
- [ ] Eigenbasis/diagonalization, repeated roots and failure to diagonalize.
- [ ] Symmetric matrices, spectral theorem for real symmetric matrices.
- [ ] Positive/semidefinite matrices and quadratic forms.
- [ ] Singular value decomposition, pseudoinverse **intro**, best rank-k approximation **statement/application**.
- [ ] Matrix conditioning, numerical sensitivity and PCA geometry **intro**.

### D4 — Applications and proof integration
- [ ] Solve linear systems by hand, then verify with NumPy.
- [ ] Derive least-squares regression and project data onto subspaces.
- [ ] Establish proofs of rank–nullity, eigenspace independence, orthogonal projection properties.
- [ ] Show how SVD informs compression and later ML.
- [ ] Distinguish data-driven observations from general linear-algebraic proofs.

## E. Multivariable and Vector Calculus — Weeks 41–51 (231 h)

### E1 — Higher-dimensional geometry and vector functions
- [ ] R^2/R^3 geometry, dot/cross products and lines/planes.
- [ ] Parametric curves and vector-valued functions, position/velocity/acceleration.
- [ ] Arc length and curvature **elementary**; coordinate changes.
- [ ] Functions of two or more variables, level sets/surfaces and domains.

### E2 — Differentiation in several variables
- [ ] Partial derivatives, higher mixed partials and differentiability.
- [ ] Tangent planes, total differential and local linear approximation.
- [ ] Directional derivatives, gradients, level curves and normal vectors.
- [ ] Multivariable chain rule; Jacobian matrices and derivatives of compositions.
- [ ] Hessian, second-derivative test and classification of critical points.
- [ ] Implicit differentiation for multivariable constraints **intro**.
- [ ] Constrained optimization and Lagrange multipliers.
- [ ] Multivariable Taylor approximation **basic**, gradient descent connection.

### E3 — Multiple integration
- [ ] Double integrals in rectangles/general regions; Fubini at calculus level.
- [ ] Polar change of variables and Jacobian absolute determinant.
- [ ] Triple integrals, cylindrical and spherical coordinates.
- [ ] General change of variables and density/center-of-mass applications.

### E4 — Vector calculus
- [ ] Vector fields, potential functions and conservative fields.
- [ ] Line integrals of scalar/vector fields and work.
- [ ] Fundamental theorem for line integrals.
- [ ] Green's theorem in plane; planar curl and circulation.
- [ ] Parametrized surfaces, normal vectors and surface integrals.
- [ ] Flux, divergence, curl and geometric interpretation.
- [ ] Divergence theorem and Stokes' theorem: **precise statements and standard computations**, selected proof ideas.
- [ ] Clear distinction between gradient/Jacobian/Hessian and divergence/curl.

## F. Discrete Mathematics and CS Foundations — Weeks 52–61 (210 h)

### F1 — Counting and combinatorics
- [ ] Sum/product principles, permutations/combinations, factorials.
- [ ] Binomial theorem and combinatorial identities; counting proofs.
- [ ] Pigeonhole principle and applications.
- [ ] Inclusion–exclusion, complements, double counting and bijections.
- [ ] Stars and bars, counting with repetition; elementary occupancy problems.
- [ ] Basic discrete probability using counting and independence **intro**; full probability Phase 2.

### F2 — Recurrences and asymptotics
- [ ] Sequences and recurrences, Fibonacci-style models.
- [ ] Solve first/second-order constant-coefficient linear recurrences **selected**.
- [ ] Ordinary generating functions **intro** for elementary sequences.
- [ ] Big-O, big-Ω, big-Θ, little-o **basic definitions and examples**.
- [ ] Growth rates of logs, polynomials and exponentials; complexity comparisons.
- [ ] Recurrence trees, substitution and Master-theorem **intro**.

### F3 — Graph theory and discrete structures
- [ ] Undirected/directed graphs, degree, walks, paths, cycles and connectivity.
- [ ] Trees, spanning trees, tree properties and proof by induction.
- [ ] Bipartite graphs, planar graphs **elementary** and Euler formula.
- [ ] Matchings, vertex coloring **selected elementary** and graph invariants.
- [ ] Graph representations, adjacency matrices and simple network models.
- [ ] Relations/partial orders revisited, state machines and invariants.

### F4 — Elementary number theory and algorithm correctness
- [ ] Divisibility, primes, Euclidean and extended Euclidean algorithms.
- [ ] Modular arithmetic, congruences, inverses and Fermat theorem **elementary**.
- [ ] Invariants and loop correctness, termination and pre-/postconditions.
- [ ] Proof of correctness for binary search, Euclidean algorithm and graph traversal **selected**.
- [ ] Breadth-first/depth-first search, elementary graph algorithms and runtime reasoning **intro**.
- [ ] Randomized-algorithm probability vocabulary **intro**; full algorithms in Phase 2.

## G. Phase 1 Consolidation — Weeks 62–64 (63 h)

- [ ] Re-derive major calculus formulas and justify theorem assumptions.
- [ ] Solve new integration and series convergence problems with method choice.
- [ ] Prove rank–nullity and basic subspace facts; explain SVD/eigen decomposition.
- [ ] Analyze a multivariable function and evaluate a multiple/line integral.
- [ ] Prove counting statements, solve recurrences and reason about graph/algorithm correctness.
- [ ] Write a fresh, closed-book cumulative qualifying exam, target ≥80%.
- [ ] Prepare Phase 2: real analysis epsilon–delta proofs, group structure, probability, ODE, numerical methods and optimization.

## What this does NOT pretend to finish

- Graduate real/complex analysis, full abstract algebra, mathematical statistics, differential equations and optimization are **later phases**.
- Phase 1 includes the **calculus-level** versions of continuity, series and vector calculus. Phase 2 repeats selected subjects rigorously, not as a redundant exercise.
- Proofs and mathematical coding are integral to all blocks rather than standalone hidden extra courses.

# Phase 1 — Exact 64-Week (1,344-Hour) Schedule

**Start:** the week after successfully completing the Phase 0 readiness gate. **One week = 7 × 3 hours = 21 hours**, inclusive of **all** reading, independent problem sets, proofs, coding and revision. Week numbers are relative, not calendar dates.

Use [TOPICS.md](TOPICS.md) for the full subtopic list and [RESOURCES.md](RESOURCES.md) for books, course URLs and the resource codes in the table below. The weekly assignments specify **what is to be finished**, at a realistic university foundation level—no expectation of completing every chapter in every optional textbook.

**Each week budget:** 6 h textbook/selected MIT lectures; 10 h written exercises and derivations/proofs; 2 h small math coding or hard problem; 3 h cumulative review and unseen test. These categories total **21 h**, not 21 h plus homework. Use Week 6, 17, 28, 40, 51, 61 and 64 for subject assessments inside the existing hours.

## Reconciliation and completion rule

| Metric | Scheduled amount |
|---|---:|
| Study blocks | **7** |
| Consecutive weeks | **64** |
| Study days (7/week) | **448** |
| Study hours (21/week) | **1,344** |

**Finishing on time** means studying all assigned topics at stated depth with representative independent problems and proofs within 64 × 21 = 1,344 scheduled hours. The target is a pacing plan, not a mathematical guarantee: repair weak prerequisites rather than falsely declaring mastery.

**Progression:** After the comprehensive Phase 1 assessment and subject-gate checks, enter [Phase 2](../Phase-2/README.md) (rigorous real analysis, abstract algebra, probability/statistics, ODEs, optimization and advanced algorithms). The entire Phase 2 depends on this groundwork, so do not omit proof training, series, linear algebra or multivariable calculus.

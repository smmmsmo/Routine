## Select exactly one specialization for the 32-week project

| Research track | Mathematical focus | Typical scoped project |
|---|---|---|
| **T1 — Optimization and mathematical ML** | Convergence bounds, convex/nonconvex objectives, stability, generalization | Re-derive and test a convergence-rate theorem under stated assumptions |
| **T2 — Algorithms and theoretical CS** | Randomization, complexity, approximation, graph algorithms | Reproduce and extend analysis of one randomized/approximation algorithm |
| **T3 — Probability and stochastic mathematics** | Concentration, martingales, Markov chains, diffusion | Investigate an example, bound or simulation for a well-defined stochastic model |
| **T4 — Analysis and applied PDE** | Functional analysis, Fourier, weak solutions, numerical approximation | Prove a restricted model result and compare with verified numerical behavior |
| **T5 — Pure algebra / number theory / geometry** | Groups, rings, modular structures, manifolds or related proofs | Explore an explicit family of objects; classify cases, prove lemmas or find counterexamples |

**Do not attempt all five tracks.** The shared skills in [TOPICS.md](TOPICS.md) are compulsory, but the *mathematics of the chosen specialty* is intentionally selected rather than spread across all fields. Ask a faculty member or experienced researcher for scope feedback when possible.

## Expected finish by Week 32

- [ ] Research problem statement, glossary, prerequisites and literature map with complete citations.
- [ ] At least **12 carefully relevant source papers** catalogued; **4–6** genuinely read in depth. Counts are planning targets, not a substitute for understanding.
- [ ] One substantive published theorem/derivation or experiment **independently reproduced** with assumptions and limitations stated.
- [ ] One rigorous investigation: proof, rigorous counterexample, verified computational study, or an honest negative result.
- [ ] A polished **12–20 page research-style report** (expository or original), mathematical notation and bibliography.
- [ ] For computational work: reproducible code, environment, seeds, tests and error/limitations analysis.
- [ ] An oral/recorded explanation or seminar presentation plus independent feedback if accessible.
- [ ] A written 6–12-month follow-on research agenda.

**Success does not require publication or a new theorem**. A correct expository thesis, reproduction, deep negative finding or independently validated extension is a legitimate research-development result. Any novelty claim must survive literature review and expert scrutiny.

## Weekly time budget (all inclusive)

- 6 h advanced reading / literature.
- 10 h theorem reconstruction, proof attempts, mathematics or careful derivation.
- 2 h computational validation, seminar preparation or difficult counterexample work.
- 3 h review, bibliography upkeep, adviser feedback/write-up.
- **Total = 21 hours per week**, including reports and experiments. Rebalance when writing intensifies; never add hidden hours to the 672-hour budget.

## Academic integrity and independent work

- Cite every borrowed claim, definition, figure, dataset, proof idea and code dependency.
- Mark **known result**, **reproduced result**, **new conjecture**, **conditional claim** and **proved contribution** separately.
- Numerical evidence is **not** universal mathematical proof.
- Formal proof assistants help validate formalized statements only when the specification faithfully matches the intended theorem.
- ArXiv/preprint posting is not automatically peer review; check author and venue policies and arXiv endorsement/eligibility where applicable.
- Research guidance is not an automated pass/fail test: seek mathematical feedback and be willing to refine the problem or extend beyond Week 32.

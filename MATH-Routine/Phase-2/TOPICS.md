# Phase 2 — Complete topic and subtopic inventory

This is the **exact scope to finish within the 72 weeks**. Study the topics in [SCHEDULE.md](SCHEDULE.md) order. Each bullet is included in the timeframe; items labelled *intro* are a bounded introduction, **not** an entire graduate course.

## A. Real Analysis I — Weeks 1–8 (168 hours)

**A1 — Rigorous foundations**
- Ordered-field axioms of real numbers; completeness axiom.
- Upper/lower bounds, maxima/minima, suprema and infima.
- Archimedean property; density of rationals and irrationals.
- Quantifiers; epsilon–N and epsilon–delta proof structure.

**A2 — Sequences and series**
- Sequence limits and uniqueness; limit laws; squeeze theorem.
- Monotone convergence theorem, bounded sequences, subsequences.
- Bolzano–Weierstrass theorem, Cauchy criterion.
- Infinite series, convergence/divergence, comparison and ratio/root tests.
- Absolute versus conditional convergence, alternating series, rearrangement awareness.

**A3 — Continuity and differentiability**
- Function limits; epsilon–delta continuity; sequential criterion.
- Intermediate value theorem, extreme value theorem, uniform continuity.
- Differentiability, derivative rules proved from limits.
- Rolle's theorem, mean value theorem, Taylor theorem with remainder.
- Applications to inequalities and rigorous approximation/error bounds.

**A4 — Rigorous integration**
- Partitions, upper/lower sums, Riemann integrability.
- Continuous functions and integrability; integrability criteria.
- Fundamental theorem of calculus; integration and limiting behavior.

## B. Real Analysis II and basic topology — Weeks 9–12 (84 hours)

**B1 — Metric-space foundations**
- Metrics, examples, open/closed balls and sets.
- Interior, closure, boundary, accumulation points.
- Compactness, connectedness, completeness; Heine–Borel in Euclidean space.
- Continuous maps between metric spaces and equivalent characterizations.

**B2 — Sequences of functions**
- Pointwise versus uniform convergence.
- Weierstrass M-test, uniform limit theorem.
- Justified exchange of limits with continuity/integration/differentiation.
- Power series and Taylor series revisited rigorously.

**B3 — Selected topology introduction**
- Topological spaces and bases: definitions and examples.
- Continuous maps, compact/connected spaces, homeomorphisms: introductory examples only.
- Full general topology is **not** claimed complete in this 4-week block.

## C. Abstract Algebra I: groups — Weeks 13–18 (126 hours)

- Binary operations; group axioms; groups, subgroups, cyclic groups.
- Order of elements and groups; permutation groups, symmetric groups.
- Homomorphisms, kernels, images and isomorphisms.
- Cosets; Lagrange theorem; normal subgroups; quotient groups.
- Isomorphism theorems for groups.
- Group actions, orbits, stabilizers, class equation.
- Cauchy's theorem; Sylow theorems **at introductory theorem/application depth**.
- Elementary examples connecting groups to symmetry and linear transformations.

## D. Abstract Algebra II: rings, fields and number theory — Weeks 19–23 (105 hours)

**D1 — Rings and fields**
- Rings with identity, commutative/noncommutative examples.
- Subrings, units, integral domains and fields.
- Ideals and quotient rings; ring homomorphisms and isomorphism theorems.
- Polynomial rings, divisibility and factorization; irreducibility.
- Euclidean domains, principal ideal domains, unique factorization domains: basic chain of implications.
- Finite fields and field extensions: introductory examples.
- **Excluded from required Phase 2 scope:** full Galois theory and advanced modules (graduate depth later).

**D2 — Selected number theory**
- Euclidean algorithm, congruences, modular arithmetic revisited rigorously.
- Chinese remainder theorem, Fermat's little theorem, Euler phi and Euler theorem.
- Primitive roots and quadratic residues: selected elementary examples.
- Diophantine equations and modular obstructions.
- Number theory is **an integrated introductory selection**, not a complete dedicated course.

## E. Probability theory — Weeks 24–31 (168 hours)

- Sample spaces, sigma-field **intuition only**; Kolmogorov axioms in elementary settings.
- Counting, conditional probability, total probability, Bayes, independence.
- Discrete/continuous random variables; PMFs, PDFs, CDFs, quantiles.
- Named distributions: Bernoulli, binomial, geometric, Poisson, uniform, exponential, normal, beta, gamma.
- Expected value, variance, covariance, correlation; indicator random variables.
- Joint/marginal/conditional distributions; independence and conditional distributions.
- Random-variable transformations; sums, Jacobian method, convolution.
- Moment and probability generating functions: introductory applications.
- Conditional expectation and law of total variance.
- Markov and Chebyshev bounds, concentration intuition.
- Law of large numbers and central limit theorem (statements, explanations, elementary applications; rigorous measure-theoretic treatment deferred).
- Elementary discrete-time Markov chains, transition matrices and stationary distributions.

## F. Mathematical Statistics — Weeks 32–38 (147 hours)

- Statistical models, random samples, statistics, likelihood.
- Sampling distributions and elementary asymptotic approximations.
- Estimation: bias, mean-square error, variance, consistency, efficiency.
- Maximum likelihood estimation, method of moments, MAP and prior/posterior connection.
- Sufficiency, factorization theorem; Rao–Blackwell introduction.
- Exponential families, Fisher information and Cramér–Rao bound introduction.
- Confidence intervals, pivotal quantities, bootstrap.
- Hypothesis tests, likelihood ratio tests, p-values and power.
- Type I/II errors, multiple testing introduction.
- Regression: simple/multiple linear models, least squares, residual diagnostics, inference.
- Bayesian versus frequentist statistical reasoning; interpretation and limitations.

## G. Differential Equations, Fourier and elementary PDEs — Weeks 39–45 (147 hours)

**G1 — ODE**
- First-order separable, linear, exact ODEs; integrating factors.
- Second-order constant coefficient linear equations; forced oscillations and resonance.
- Systems of first-order ODEs; matrix exponential and eigenvalue methods.
- Existence/uniqueness ideas; local stability, equilibria, phase portraits.
- Laplace transforms and solutions to initial-value problems.

**G2 — Fourier methods and PDE introduction**
- Fourier series of periodic functions: orthogonality and coefficients.
- Convergence intuition, even/odd extensions.
- Heat and wave equations; separation of variables for simple boundary conditions.
- Introductory Fourier transform, frequency-domain meaning and convolution.
- These are **selected mathematical methods**, not full PDE/harmonic-analysis courses.

## H. Numerical Analysis — Weeks 46–51 (126 hours)

- Floating-point arithmetic, roundoff, truncation, conditioning and stability.
- Error orders, convergence rates and stopping criteria.
- Root finding: bisection, Newton, secant methods.
- Polynomial interpolation, Lagrange/Newton forms, interpolation error.
- Numerical differentiation and quadrature; trapezoidal/Simpson rules.
- Numerical linear algebra: conditioning, LU/QR, iterative methods and least squares.
- Numerical ODE integration: Euler and Runge–Kutta; stability intuition.
- Numerical experiments and reproducible error analysis.

## I. Convex and Numerical Optimization — Weeks 52–57 (126 hours)

- Optimization models, objective/constraint definitions.
- Convex sets, convex functions, epigraphs and basic convexity proofs.
- First/second order conditions; gradients, Hessians, curvature and conditioning.
- Unconstrained methods: gradient descent, backtracking, Newton/quasi-Newton.
- Constrained optimization, Lagrange multipliers, KKT conditions.
- Lagrange duality, weak/strong duality and Slater condition introduction.
- Linear programming and quadratic programming examples.
- Stochastic gradient descent and mini-batch noise; convergence intuition.
- Regularization, least squares and logistic-loss optimization connections.

## J. Algorithm Design and Analysis — Weeks 58–64 (147 hours)

- Asymptotic notation, recurrence methods and performance proofs (review + advance).
- Divide-and-conquer and master theorem; randomized partition algorithms.
- Greedy algorithms, exchange arguments, cut/cycle properties.
- Dynamic programming, subproblem structure, correctness.
- Graph algorithms: BFS/DFS review, MST, shortest paths, topological order.
- Amortized analysis: aggregate, accounting, potential methods.
- Flow networks, max-flow/min-cut theorem and matching connection.
- Randomized algorithms and expectation-based analysis.
- Approximation algorithms; approximation ratio and proof.
- Reductions and introduction to NP-completeness **only as an application**; full complexity theory belongs to Phase 3.

## K. Complex Analysis — Weeks 65–69 (105 hours)

- Complex numbers, geometry and topology of the complex plane.
- Complex limits, derivatives, holomorphic functions and Cauchy–Riemann equations.
- Elementary transcendental complex functions, branches and logarithm.
- Contour/line integrals, Cauchy's theorem and integral formula.
- Analyticity and Taylor series.
- Laurent series, isolated singularities, residues.
- Residue theorem and real integral applications.
- Conformal mappings: introductory examples.
- Complete advanced continuation/Riemann surfaces are **outside** Phase 2.

## L. Consolidation — Weeks 70–72 (63 hours)

- Integrate rigorous proof skills in real analysis and algebra.
- Re-solve unseen probability/statistics and differential equation questions.
- Analyze numerical methods and optimization under stated assumptions.
- Construct and analyze nontrivial algorithm proofs and running-time bounds.
- Compare major connections: completeness–convergence; groups–modular arithmetic; probability–inference; ODE–Fourier; linear algebra–optimization.
- Complete fresh cumulative written assessments and repair remaining prerequisite gaps.

## Course boundaries

The words **'complete Phase 2'** mean mastery of the subtopics above, at the level specified. It does **not** mean the entire contents of Abbott, Artin, Casella–Berger, Rudin, Munkres, Ahlfors, Nocedal–Wright and CLRS. That would exceed a 72-week / 1,512-hour self-study plan. Higher-order treatments are reserved for later specialization.

# Phase 0 — Best-Fit Books and Resources

**Priority: teaching quality, depth and exercise quality, not price.** Your university may provide paid textbooks, so use the best fit regardless of cost. You need **one main spine, selected challenge sources, and optional visual support**—not every book at once.

| Code | Role / weeks | Primary resource | Why / exact use in Phase 0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **PC** | **Primary spine: Weeks 1–13** | **James Stewart, Lothar Redlin & Saleem Watson — *Precalculus: Mathematics for Calculus*, 8th ed.** ([Cengage publisher](https://www.cengage.com/c/precalculus-8e-stewart-redlin-watson/9780357753637/)) | **Main book throughout Phase 0.** Fundamentals and algebra (Weeks 1–4), functions (5), exponential/logarithmic functions (6–7), trigonometry (8–9), sequences/vectors/conics (10–11), limit preview (12). Read by topic and solve representative exercises, not the whole 1,000-page text. |
| **LANG** | Conceptual repair: Weeks 1–9 **only when needed** | **Serge Lang — *Basic Mathematics*** ([Springer publisher](https://link.springer.com/book/9780387967875)) | More careful conceptual explanations for forgotten arithmetic, algebra, geometry and trig. Use to repair a specific gap; not a mandatory second full book. |
| **AOPS-A** | Optional algebra challenge: Weeks 2–7 | **Richard Rusczyk & Mathew Crawford — *Intermediate Algebra*** ([AoPS publisher](https://artofproblemsolving.com/store/book/intermediate-algebra)) | Deep unfamiliar algebra, polynomial, inequality, equation, log and function exercises. Choose **selected problems**, after core mechanics are reliable. |
| **AOPS-P** | Optional trig/vector challenge: Weeks 8–11 | **Richard Rusczyk — *Precalculus*** ([AoPS publisher](https://artofproblemsolving.com/store/book/precalculus)) | Challenge questions in trig, complex numbers and vectors. Not a second complete precalculus course. |
| **GELF** | Optional insight: Weeks 5–9 | **I. M. Gelfand & A. Shen — *Algebra*** ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/9780817636777)); **I. M. Gelfand & M. Saul — *Trigonometry*** ([Springer](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4612-0149-6)) | Elegantly develop ideas instead of memorizing log/trig identities. Select **one relevant concept or problem at a time**. |
| **MIT** | Calculus readiness: Weeks 12–13 | **MIT 18.01SC — Single Variable Calculus** ([MIT OCW](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/)) | First limit/differentiation lessons and selected exercises **only**. Full Calculus I/II belongs to Phase 1. |
| **PROOF** | Proof literacy: Week 14 | **Daniel J. Velleman — *How to Prove It*, 3rd ed.** ([Cambridge publisher](https://www.cambridge.org/highereducation/books/how-to-prove-it/6D2965D625C6836CD4A785A2C843B3DA)) | Sample the first sections on logic, statements, sets and elementary proof techniques. **Phase 1** contains the complete proof foundation course. |
| **REV** | Cumulative Weeks 15–16 | Earlier **PC, LANG, MIT, PROOF** selections, with **unseen** mixed textbook exercises | No new mandatory book: consolidate, re-test logarithms and trig, solve integrated problems and check Phase 1 readiness. |

## Equally legitimate free resources where helpful

- **OpenStax *Algebra and Trigonometry*, 2e** — [official book](https://openstax.org/books/algebra-and-trigonometry-2e/pages/1-introduction-to-prerequisites). Alternative full-course algebra/trig text and practice.
- **OpenStax *Precalculus*, 2e** — [official book](https://openstax.org/books/precalculus-2e/pages/1-introduction-to-functions). Alternative to Stewart if preferred.
- **Khan Academy — Trigonometry** — [official course](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/trigonometry). Unit-circle, graphs, identity and equation drills for Weeks 8–9.
- **Richard Hammack — *Book of Proof*** — [author edition](https://richardhammack.github.io/BookOfProof/). Alternative to Velleman for Week 14.
- **3Blue1Brown — *Essence of Calculus*** — [author site](https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/calculus). Visualization for Weeks 12–13; not sufficient on its own.
- **Desmos Graphing Calculator** — [tool](https://www.desmos.com/calculator). Verify graphs *after* predicting domain and graph shape.

## Exact resource timeline

| Weeks | Code(s) | Primary reading / exercise topics |
|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | PC, LANG when weak | Fundamentals: arithmetic, exponents, linear equations, inequalities, systems |
| 3–4 | PC, AOPS-A optional | Polynomial/quadratic/rational/radical equations and sign charts |
| 5 | PC, GELF optional | Functions, inverse/composition, transformations and graphs |
| 6–7 | PC, AOPS-A optional | Exponential/log rules, equations, graphs, inequalities and modeling |
| 8–9 | PC, AOPS-P optional | Geometry, unit circle, trig identities, equations and graphs |
| 10–11 | PC, AOPS-P optional | Sequences, sigma notation, vectors, parametric/polar intro, modeling |
| 12–13 | PC, MIT | Limit/continuity preview, derivative difference quotient, integration intuition |
| 14 | PROOF | First elementary logic, sets, proof/counterexample and induction topics |
| 15–16 | REV | Cumulative mixed practice and skill transfer; **no new textbook** |

## Study/use instructions in the scheduled hours

- **6 hours/week** focused reading and key worked examples from the main resource.
- **10 hours/week** independent textbook problems, derivations, explanations.
- **2 hours/week** challenge problems or a brief plotted/numerical exploration.
- **3 hours/week** spaced review and closed-book mixed exercises.
- **Total = 21 hours/week**, including all exercises and checking. No additional hidden assignment hours.

**Edition note:** If the university holds the 7th rather than 8th Stewart edition, study the same *topic names*, since section/chapter numbering may differ. For library access, prefer an authorized eBook or print copy. No need to buy all optional references.

**Rule:** Finish the specific assigned [weekly topics](SCHEDULE.md), not every exercise or every page of all recommended books. Deep proof-based calculus and linear algebra are deliberately Phase 1.

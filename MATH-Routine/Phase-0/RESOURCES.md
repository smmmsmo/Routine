# Phase 0 — Resources and exact study directions

Use **one main textbook + optional reinforcement**, not ten courses at once. These are *resources*, not a demand to finish the entire books during Phase 0.

## Primary resources, in order

1. **OpenStax: Algebra and Trigonometry, 2e (free)** — [textbook](https://openstax.org/books/algebra-and-trigonometry-2e/pages/1-introduction-to-prerequisites). Main school-math revision text: Ch. 1 prerequisites; Ch. 2 equations/inequalities; Ch. 3–6 functions/logs; Ch. 7–10 trig; Ch. 11–13 selected algebra, coordinate geometry and sequences. This is the **default for Weeks 1–11**.
2. **OpenStax: Precalculus, 2e (free)** — [textbook](https://openstax.org/books/precalculus-2e/pages/1-introduction-to-functions). Alternative/reference for functions, log/exponential models, unit circle, sequences. Do **not** duplicate every exercise between the two books.
3. **Khan Academy: Trigonometry (free)** — [course](https://www.khanacademy.org/math/trigonometry). Use quizzes for unit-circle and identity gaps, especially Weeks 8–9.
4. **Book of Proof by Richard Hammack (free PDF available from author)** — [official site](https://richardhammack.github.io/BookOfProof/). Phase 0: select parts of Ch. 1–2 (sets and logic), 4–6 (basic proof styles) and 10 (induction); deeper chapters belong in Phase 1.
5. **MIT OCW 18.01SC: Single Variable Calculus (free)** — [course](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/). During Phase 0 only: use the **opening differentiation and introductory limit material** as reinforcement. The full course is reserved for Phase 1.

## Helpful tools, not substitutes for independent work

- [Desmos graphing calculator](https://www.desmos.com/calculator): graphs/transformations; predict a graph before checking.
- [GeoGebra graphing](https://www.geogebra.org/graphing): geometry, trig and function explorations.
- [3Blue1Brown — Essence of Calculus](https://www.3blue1brown.com/topics/calculus): intuition, especially Weeks 12–13.
- [Python documentation](https://docs.python.org/3/tutorial/): Python refresh.
- [NumPy documentation](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/): vectors and numerical arrays.
- [Matplotlib tutorials](https://matplotlib.org/stable/tutorials/index.html): graphing.
- [SymPy documentation](https://docs.sympy.org/latest/index.html): verify symbolic work *after* manual derivation.

## How to use a textbook section

1. Do the prior chapter's prerequisite check or attempt 3 diagnostic questions.
2. Read definitions actively. Make one simple example and one counterexample where appropriate.
3. Close the book and reconstruct the central rule or argument.
4. Solve 5 routine and 3 mixed problems **before** looking at worked solutions.
5. Check carefully, rewrite errors in the error log, then revisit after 2/7/21 days.
6. Explain a connection: e.g., logarithms ↔ exponential inverse; trig ↔ unit circle; limits ↔ derivative.

## Why these choices?

- OpenStax's Algebra and Trigonometry has a clear prerequisites → equations → functions → logarithms → trigonometry progression.
- Khan Academy offers independent trig exercises and mastery quizzes.
- Hammack's book supplies an approachable proof-writing introduction.
- MIT 18.01SC is a genuine university course with exercises and exams, but assumes high-school algebra/trigonometry; therefore use selectively until the Phase 0 gate.

## Book spending rule

Try the free core first. Buy a print copy if it meaningfully improves attention, not because paid books are necessary to progress.

# Phase 0 — Complete Foundation & Revision Topics

**Complete these topics within 16 weeks / 336 scheduled study hours**, following [SCHEDULE.md](SCHEDULE.md). Starting point: school mathematics and introductory calculus were studied before but much has been forgotten, especially **logarithms and trigonometry**. This is **revision + mastery**, not a new course in real analysis or calculus I.

Study the named items to the *school-to-university readiness level*. Selected introductions are deliberately bounded; their full treatments come in Phase 1.

## A. Number Sense, Algebra & Equations — Weeks 1–4 (84 hours)

### A1. Number systems, arithmetic, measurement
- [ ] Natural numbers, integers, rational and irrational numbers, real number line and intervals.
- [ ] Signed operations, order of operations and absolute values.
- [ ] Fractions (all four operations), decimals, recurring decimals and percentage changes.
- [ ] Ratios, proportions, rates, units, unit conversions and dimensional consistency.
- [ ] Factors, multiples, primes, divisibility, gcd/lcm and factorization.
- [ ] Estimation, rounding, significant figures and scientific notation.

### A2. Algebraic expressions
- [ ] Algebraic properties, like terms, expanding and simplifying.
- [ ] Exponents: integer, negative, zero and rational powers; laws and domain conditions.
- [ ] Radicals, simplification, conjugates and rationalizing denominators.
- [ ] Polynomial expressions, factoring, difference of squares, sum/difference of cubes.
- [ ] Rational expressions: common denominators, cancellation and restrictions.
- [ ] Simple complex numbers: a+bi, conjugate, arithmetic and modulus (intro).

### A3. Equations, systems, inequalities
- [ ] Linear equations; rearrange formulas and translate word problems.
- [ ] Linear inequalities, compound inequalities and interval notation.
- [ ] Absolute-value equations and inequalities.
- [ ] Two-variable linear systems by substitution and elimination.
- [ ] Quadratics: factoring, square completion, quadratic formula and discriminant.
- [ ] Polynomial zeros, factor/remainder theorems (intro), synthetic division (intro).
- [ ] Rational and radical equations; extraneous roots and prohibited inputs.
- [ ] Polynomial/rational inequalities; sign charts and graphical meaning.

## B. Functions, Logarithms, Geometry & Trigonometry — Weeks 5–9 (105 hours)

### B1. Functions and graphs
- [ ] Relations vs functions, notation, domain, codomain, range.
- [ ] Evaluation, zeroes/intercepts, piecewise definitions and discontinuity from graphs.
- [ ] Graph transformations: horizontal/vertical shifts, scaling, reflections.
- [ ] Composition and inverse functions; when inverses exist.
- [ ] Linear, quadratic, polynomial and rational families; symmetry/monotonicity.
- [ ] Vertical/horizontal asymptotes, restrictions, end behavior.
- [ ] Simple mathematical modeling and translating among words/tables/equations/graphs.

### B2. Exponentials and logarithms — **must become fluent**
- [ ] Exponential functions a^x, a>0, a≠1; e^x, growth/decay.
- [ ] Logarithm as inverse: log_a(x), domain x>0 and base restrictions.
- [ ] Derive product, quotient, power laws and change of base.
- [ ] Natural/common logs; convert exponential ↔ logarithmic equations.
- [ ] Simplify logarithmic expressions with correct domains.
- [ ] Solve exponential equations, logarithmic equations and related inequalities.
- [ ] Identify and reject extraneous logarithmic solutions.
- [ ] Graph exponential/log families, inverses and asymptotes.
- [ ] Growth/half-life/doubling time/compound-interest modeling.
- [ ] Explain **why log(x+y) ≠ log(x)+log(y)** generally.

### B3. Geometry and coordinate geometry
- [ ] Angles, triangles, congruence, similarity, Pythagoras and basic Euclidean reasoning.
- [ ] Circles, perimeter/area, basic surface area/volume.
- [ ] Coordinates, distance, midpoint, slope, line equations and intersections.
- [ ] Circles in the plane, parabola and conics (intro).
- [ ] Geometric interpretation of equations and inequalities.

### B4. Trigonometry — **must become fluent**
- [ ] Degrees, radians, arc length, unit circle and signs by quadrant.
- [ ] Right-triangle sine, cosine, tangent and reciprocal functions.
- [ ] Unit-circle exact values at common angles in all quadrants.
- [ ] Trig function graphs: amplitude, period, phase shifts, translations.
- [ ] Pythagorean, quotient, reciprocal, even/odd identities.
- [ ] Sum/difference and double-angle identities; selected half-angle identities (intro).
- [ ] Derive and simplify identities without using the statement being proved.
- [ ] Trig equations on finite intervals and general solution families.
- [ ] Inverse trig functions, principal values, domains and ranges.
- [ ] Law of sines, law of cosines, triangle area and applied problems.
- [ ] Connect trig functions to coordinates and vectors.

## C. Precalculus & Calculus Readiness — Weeks 10–13 (84 hours)

### C1. Sequences, vectors and representations
- [ ] Arithmetic and geometric sequences and sums.
- [ ] Sigma notation, finite sums and binomial expansion (intro).
- [ ] Two-dimensional vectors, components, length, direction, addition and dot product intuition.
- [ ] Parametric equations and polar coordinates (intro).
- [ ] Compare linear, polynomial, exponential and logarithmic growth in models.

### C2. Limits and continuity (introductory)
- [ ] Limits from tables, graphs and algebra; one-sided and infinite limits.
- [ ] Limit laws, substitution, factoring and rationalization; indeterminate 0/0 interpretation.
- [ ] Continuity, removable/jump/infinite discontinuity and piecewise continuity.
- [ ] Average vs instantaneous rate and tangent-line intuition.
- [ ] Difference quotient and derivative from first principles in elementary examples.

### C3. Derivative and integral previews (do not duplicate Phase 1)
- [ ] Basic derivatives: power, product, quotient and chain rule **introductory practice**.
- [ ] Derivatives of sin, cos, exp and ln in simple examples.
- [ ] Tangent lines, local change, critical points and simple optimization.
- [ ] Antiderivatives, area, Riemann-sum intuition and numerical approximation.
- [ ] Explain how a limit defines the derivative; why the integral measures accumulation.
- [ ] Distinguish Phase 0 readiness from full Calculus I/II.

## D. Mathematical Reasoning & Integration — Weeks 14–16 (63 hours)

### D1. Entry-level proof language
- [ ] Sets, subset and element notation; union/intersection/complement.
- [ ] Statements and logical operators, implications and negations.
- [ ] Universal/existential quantifiers **introductory use**.
- [ ] Direct proof, contrapositive, contradiction and counterexample **basic examples**.
- [ ] Induction: base case, hypothesis, step for elementary sums.
- [ ] Write coherent mathematical explanations; detect a false step or missing domain.
- [ ] **Full systematic proof course occurs in Phase 1**, not Phase 0.

### D2. Mathematical computing / integration
- [ ] Plot valid-domain algebraic, exponential, logarithmic and trig graphs with a tool (Desmos/Python).
- [ ] Check a derivative or simple limit numerically; know that computation is not proof.
- [ ] Distinguish approximation, exact answer, boundary-case and invalid input.
- [ ] Solve mixed problems without chapter labels: algebra ↔ functions ↔ logs ↔ trig ↔ limits.
- [ ] Explain formulas from first principles where feasible, rather than reciting them.

### D3. Cumulative qualification
- [ ] Repair every essential error in algebra, logarithms, trigonometry and functions.
- [ ] Solve a fresh mixed set unaided; overall target ≥80% and ≥70% in each key category.
- [ ] Correctly explain 2–3 elementary results/proofs in mathematical sentences.
- [ ] Show **7-day retention** on at least 10 representative unseen questions **within Week 16**.
- [ ] Demonstrate readiness for Phase 1: proof foundations, calculus, linear algebra and discrete math.

## Boundaries

This phase does **not** attempt full university calculus, rigorous analysis, abstract linear algebra, graph theory, probability, optimization or research papers. Those begin in later phases. If a subtopic is already fluent on an unseen assessment, allocate its allotted time to forgotten items and deeper mixed problems; don't count recognition of a formula as mastery.

# Phase 0 — Exact 16-Week Schedule (336 Hours)

**Start here after scheduling three hours daily.** Phase 0 is **school-mathematics revision and calculus/proof readiness**, not a full university calculus course. Week 1 begins with a *closed-book diagnostic*. The 16-week timeframe contains **all required topics from [TOPICS.md](TOPICS.md)**. See [RESOURCES.md](RESOURCES.md) for exact books/lecture links and resource codes.

**Every week has 21 hours (7 days × 3 h):** roughly 6 h core reading/examples, 10 h independent exercises, 2 h harder questions or math visualization, 3 h mixed revision and recall. Every seventh day is a 3-hour assessment/review session. **Reading, homework and review are all included**; no additional hours. Aim for substantial unseen exercises and track errors, with extra mixed work on forgotten log and trig identities. If a weekly skill is already strong, use its hours for weaker prerequisites, not unnecessary repeated video watching.

## Week 1 diagnostic and Week 16 gate (within 336 hours)

- **Week 1:** spend the first 2–3 study hours on an unseen no-notes test covering fractions, exponent laws, equations/inequalities, function domains/inverses, logarithms, common unit-circle values and basic limits. Mark each topic red/yellow/green to shift the **allocated hours** within the 16-week schedule.
- **Week 4:** algebra mixed check before functions.
- **Week 7:** logarithms and exponentials mixed check, including domain errors.
- **Week 9:** unit-circle, trig identities, graphs/equations check.
- **Week 13:** precalculus readiness check: modeling, limits, continuity and derivative preview.
- **Week 15:** unseen full mock and mark weaknesses.
- **Week 16:** ≥7 days after the mock, re-test a fresh selection from the same skills (**retention inside the 16-week timeframe**) and complete the final mixed test. Aim for **≥80%** overall, **≥70%** for each essential algebra/functions/logarithms/trigonometry category, plus a correct short proof explanation. If not met, extend the calendar instead of declaring completion.

## Phase-to-phase boundary

What is completed here: high-school algebra/functions/trigonometry, logarithms, pre-calculus sequences/vectors, calculus readiness, elementary proof language. **Phase 1 then starts** rigorous systematic proofs, Calculus I–II, linear algebra, multivariable calculus and discrete mathematics. Topic previews in Weeks 12–14 are not a substitute for Phase 1.

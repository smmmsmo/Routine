# Phase 3 — Best-fit books and university resources

**Quality-first, not price-first.** Paid books are welcome because university library access is available. Choose **one primary spine for each block**, and use secondary sources only when proof depth, exercises or clarification demand it. Resources are selected for mathematical rigor and their fit to the **specific scoped Phase 3 topics**, not for covering every page of each source.

## How to use these resources within the fixed timeline

- **6 hours/week:** primary reading, selective lectures, worked examples.
- **10 hours/week:** textbook problems, proof reconstruction and mathematical derivation.
- **2 hours/week:** a small experiment/algorithm implementation or one particularly difficult problem.
- **3 hours/week:** cumulative mixed review and closed-book quiz.
- Total **21 hours/week**. This includes coursework, not coursework + extra hours.

**Do not** try to finish every resource or every section. A primary book may span 1,000+ pages; Phase 3 assigns **specific topics only**, not a complete read of that text.

## Selection notes

- **ML theory:** Shalev-Shwartz/Ben-David is more appropriate for proofs than a purely API-centric ML tutorial.
- **Probabilistic modeling:** Murphy Advanced unifies Bayesian inference, graphical models and deep generative models; CMU adds structured lectures and assignments.
- **Deep learning:** Prince has equation-level explanations and published exercise notebooks, with Goodfellow as a deeper foundation reference.
- **Language models:** Stanford CS336's full course includes expensive systems engineering; we only need relevant transformer mathematics here.
- **Theory of CS:** Sipser and MIT 6.045J provide proof-first explanations of decidability, reductions and complexity.
- **Information theory:** Cover–Thomas is an established rigorous text; we use selected fundamental chapters rather than all its network information theory.

**Availability:** Texts can be borrowed through the university. A paid resource is not required if a rigorous, author-provided edition exists legally. The advanced AI/CS field changes; re-check modern course material when you actually reach these weeks.
